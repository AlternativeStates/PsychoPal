The "dark magic mushroom" co-produces amatoxins and psilocybin.

Andrew R Kunik, Michael W Christopher, Benjamin Lemmond, Bryn T M Dentinger, Jason C Slot, Timothy J Garrett, Matthew E Smith
Current biology : CB · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.1016/j.cub.2026.10.041
PsilocybinPeer reviewed#psilocybin#fungi#horizontal gene transfer

Aim To determine whether the mushroom Galerina indica produces both psilocybin and amatoxins.

Methods Mass spectrometry of mushroom tissue to detect psilocybin and amatoxins, genomic analysis to identify biosynthetic genes, and phylogenetic analysis to infer their evolutionary origin.

Results Psilocybin and amatoxins were both detected, with corresponding biosynthetic genes identified. Phylogenetic evidence suggests psilocybin biosynthesis was acquired by horizontal gene transfer after amatoxin biosynthesis was established.

Conclusion Horizontal gene transfer can combine bioactive systems in one species, potentially altering the ecological roles and fitness of both compound classes.

Why it matters for you: Of limited direct relevance to your PET or neurodynamics work, though it touches psilocybin chemistry you may find of interest.
