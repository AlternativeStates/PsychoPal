Synergistic contributions of periodic and aperiodic cortical activity to motor recovery prediction after stroke.

Seitaro Iwama, Daisuke Ito, Takayuki Kamimoto, Arisa Kawabata, Junsuke Ito, Tetsuya Tsuji, Junichi Ushiba, Michiyuki Kawakami
Brain communications · 2026-10-21 · doi:10.1093/braincomms/fcag400
EEGClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformInformation theory / complexityComputational / whole-brain modellingMachine learning / AI#aperiodic activity#eeg#stroke

Aim To test whether periodic and aperiodic resting EEG components predict motor recovery after chronic stroke.

Methods Resting-state EEG (1 minute) recorded at admission and discharge with a portable system in 41 chronic stroke patients, parameterised with spectral decomposition and used in stepwise regression and classification.

Results Ipsilesional alpha-peak activity increased after rehabilitation and correlated with motor gain (r = 0.39). A model of higher individual alpha frequency, steeper aperiodic slope and their interaction predicted recovery (R2 = 0.53; balanced accuracy 88%) and outperformed a delta-alpha ratio.

Conclusion The interaction between periodic and aperiodic activity is a mechanistically meaningful and clinically relevant marker of residual plasticity in chronic stroke.

Why it matters for you: Directly engages your interest in aperiodic activity and separating oscillatory from broadband EEG components.
