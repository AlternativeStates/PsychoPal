Psychedelic-assisted therapies (Psilocybin and MDMA) for treatment-resistant psychiatric disorders

Maria Luiza Góes Gurgel, Ana Luísa Carrijo Ferreira, Luisa da Costa Mello, Amanda Comandolli Caldato, Sofia Zaher do Canto, Pietra Brossi Camilotti, Manuela Ruela Folino Silva, Raissa Assalin Gomes, Beatriz Bomfim Wiltembürg, Giulia Spadone Perini, Manoela Rosa Bertipaglia, Rafaella Barcelos de Matos, Enzo Seletti Orsi, Fernanda Oliveira Nacle, Gabriela Fernandes Daniel Ribeiro
International Health Sciences Review · 2026-11-10 · doi:10.70164/ihsr.v2i6.268
PsilocybinDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)PTSDClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#psilocybin#mdma#psychedelic therapy

Aim To describe the mechanisms, efficacy and safety of psilocybin- and MDMA-assisted therapy for depression and PTSD, and to critically examine evidence limitations.

Methods Narrative, non-systematic review of PubMed/MEDLINE literature from 2016 onwards, including randomised trials, systematic reviews, meta-analyses and mechanistic and safety studies; twelve references were qualitatively synthesised.

Results Psilocybin reduced depressive symptoms with a moderate pooled effect (Hedges' g = 0.66), with limited evidence specific to treatment-resistant depression. MDMA-assisted therapy reduced PTSD severity in two phase 3 trials, with transient adverse effects common and serious events reported in people with neuropsychiatric conditions.

Conclusion Psychedelic-assisted therapies are promising but methodological limits, safety uncertainties and unclear long-term efficacy warrant caution before wider implementation.

Why it matters for you: Summarises the psilocybin depression evidence base that your clinical pharmacology and psilocybin work sits within.
