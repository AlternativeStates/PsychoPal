An examination of cerebellar perineuronal nets in major depression and child abuse

Refilwe Mpai, Reza Rahimian, Gohar Fakhfouri, Lena Hug, Julia Alcaide, Claudia Belliveau, Maria Antonietta Davoli, Gustavo Turecki, Naguib Mechawar
Frontiers in Synaptic Neuroscience · 2026-11-25 · doi:10.3389/fnsyn.2026.1981015
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewed#perineuronal nets#child abuse#cerebellum

Aim Test whether a history of child abuse alters perineuronal nets in the human dentate nucleus and in mouse deep cerebellar nuclei.

Methods Post-mortem human dentate nucleus from depressed suicides with or without child abuse history and matched controls, using FISH and immunofluorescence; mice reared under limited-bedding early-life adversity versus standard rearing.

Results Perineuronal net density was unchanged by child abuse in humans and by early-life adversity in mice, and the phenotype of enveloped cells did not differ in the human dentate nucleus.

Conclusion Cerebellar perineuronal nets, which mature early postnatally, do not appear to be affected by child abuse, in contrast to prefrontal findings.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your interest in neuroplasticity markers, though it addresses cerebellar nets rather than the PET or EEG measures you use.
