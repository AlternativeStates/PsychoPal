Cost-effectiveness of psychedelic-assisted therapies for depression and post-traumatic stress disorder: A systematic review.

Mohammad Veysi Sheikhrobat, Nasim Jaberi, Ahmad Tahmasebi-Ghorrabi, Monireh Shamsaei
Psychiatry research · 2026-11-13 · doi:10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117596
PsilocybinDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Substance use (other)PTSDPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#psychedelics#cost-effectiveness#ptsd

Aim To systematically review the cost-effectiveness of psychedelic-assisted therapies for depression and PTSD.

Methods PRISMA 2020 systematic review of four databases; seven full economic evaluations assessed with the QHES instrument and ICERs converted to 2024 US dollars.

Results Five studies evaluated MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD and two evaluated psilocybin-assisted therapy for severe or treatment-resistant depression. MDMA-assisted therapy was generally cost-effective in high-income settings, while evidence for psilocybin was limited and mixed.

Conclusion Psychedelic-assisted therapy can generate economic value for PTSD, but cost-effectiveness for depression remains uncertain and context dependent, requiring more evaluations.

Why it matters for you: Useful context for your psychedelic clinical pharmacology and psilocybin-for-depression work, especially on health economics and implementation.
