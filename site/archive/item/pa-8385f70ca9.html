Effects of combined palmitoylethanolamide and probiotics on behavior and hippocampal and colonic gene expression in 12-month-old mice exposed to chronic stress.

Anne Abot, Sophie Holowacz, Elsa Jacouton
Frontiers in nutrition · 2026-11-09 · doi:10.3389/fnut.2026.1912894
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)AnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling#palmitoylethanolamide#probiotics#gut-brain axis

Aim To evaluate combined palmitoylethanolamide and probiotic supplementation on behaviour and hippocampal and colonic gene expression in middle-aged mice exposed to chronic stress.

Methods Male C57BL/6J mice aged 12 months given PEA (5 mg/kg) plus three probiotic strains or vehicle for 10 weeks; 8-week chronic unpredictable mild stress; behavioural tests and gene expression in colon and hippocampus.

Results Supplementation reduced some stress-induced anxiety-like behaviours and increased locomotion, moderated corticosterone rises, upregulated hippocampal Nr3c1, decreased Il6, and increased Bdnf, Syn1, Syp, Gfap and Iba1 expression, with colonic changes in Ocln and Il10.

Conclusion Behavioural gains were limited, but coordinated peripheral and central transcriptional changes support further study as an approach to late-life depression-related alterations.

Why it matters for you: Connects gut-brain signalling and hippocampal plasticity markers, overlapping your interest in brain-body coupling and neuroplasticity.
