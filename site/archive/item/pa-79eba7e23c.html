An engineered cohesion system reduces errors in aged mammalian eggs.

Alexandre Webster, Halima Yusuf, Nina Schweizer, Chloe Charalambous, Eike Urs Mönnich, Heike Eckel, Rüdiger Moltrecht, Martyn Blayney, Kay Elder, Claus Sibold, Andreas Tandler-Schneider, Melina Schuh
Science (New York, N.Y.) · 2026-11-26 · doi:10.1126/science.aei5852
Pre-clinicalPeer reviewed#oocytes#aneuploidy#cohesin

Aim Whether an engineered artificial cohesion system can reduce premature sister chromatid separation in aged mammalian eggs.

Methods Creation of an artificial cohesion system in aged mouse eggs, with a separase-cleavable sequence for scheduled deactivation, plus a proof-of-principle test tethering human chromosomes.

Results The artificial cohesion system reduced premature sister chromatid separation in aged mouse eggs and could be adapted to tether human chromosomes.

Conclusion Provides a proof of principle for future approaches to reduce premature separation in eggs from women of advanced reproductive age.
