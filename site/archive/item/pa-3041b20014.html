Intermittent theta burst stimulation to extend early ketamine response in treatment-resistant depression: A preliminary enriched randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial.

Tung-Ping Su, Mao-Hsuan Huang, Wan-Chen Chang, Wen-Han Chang, Pei-Chi Tu, Wei-Shan Huang, Wei-Chung Mao, Mu-Hong Chen
Psychiatry and clinical neurosciences · 2026-11-04 · doi:10.1111/pcn.70165
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain stimulationClinical trial / evidence synthesis

Background Ketamine produces rapid antidepressant effects in treatment-resistant depression (TRD), but these benefits diminish within days.

Methods Adults with TRD who exhibited an antidepressant response to two low-dose ketamine infusions (0.5 mg/kg) were randomized to 5 days of accelerated iTBS or sham stimulation.

Results In the intention-to-treat analysis, the Phase 2 mixed-effects model showed a significant overall group effect favoring active iTBS (P = 0.020), without a significant group × time interaction. Exploratory remission rates were higher with active iTBS at Days 15 and 29.

Conclusion In this enriched trial, ketamine infusion followed by iTBS was associated with lower overall depressive symptom severity during the randomized follow-up period through Day 49 among early ketamine responders.
