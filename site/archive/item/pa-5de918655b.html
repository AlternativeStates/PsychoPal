Psilocybin induces delayed and dynamic changes in human sleep neurophysiology: Results from a randomized controlled trial of psilocybin assisted therapy in patients with comorbid major depressive and alcohol use disorder

Matthew J. Reid, Eli Weissman, Luis Luna Martinez, Zarmeen Zahid, Caitlin M. DuPont, Hongmin Dai, Patrick H. Finan, Frederick Streeter Barrett
Research Square · 2026-11-17 · doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-11333812/v1
PsilocybinDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Alcohol use disorderClinicalPreprintComputational / whole-brain modellingClinical trial / evidence synthesis#psilocybin#sleep eeg#alcohol use disorder

Aim Characterise the acute and post-acute temporal dynamics of sleep neurophysiology after psilocybin-assisted therapy in comorbid depression and alcohol use disorder.

Methods Randomised placebo-controlled trial with 40 participants (psilocybin n=22, placebo n=18), home sleep EEG for three nights before and seven nights after administration, analysed with nonlinear mixed-effects models.

Results Delayed, nonlinear changes emerged after psilocybin, with NREM slow-wave activity, sigma and REM beta peaking or troughing around 3–4 nights post-administration. Sleep efficiency and total sleep time increased over the first three post-treatment nights, while architecture changes were largely absent.

Conclusion Psilocybin-assisted therapy induces delayed, dynamic sleep EEG changes beyond the acute period, suggesting sleep EEG may be a sensitive measure of evolving neurophysiology.

Why it matters for you: Home EEG tracking of delayed neurophysiological effects after psilocybin links directly to your EEG and psychedelic clinical pharmacology work.
