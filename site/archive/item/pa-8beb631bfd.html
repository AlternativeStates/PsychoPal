Community and EMS Interventions to Improve Survival in Cardiac Arrest.

Carolina Malta Hansen, Konstantin A Krychtiuk, Monique A Starks, Hussein R Al-Khalidi, Daniel B Mark, Lisa Monk, Lisa Kaltenbach, James G Jollis, Sana M Al-Khatib, Hayden B Bosworth, Kimberly Ward, Sarah Smith, Tyler Cope, Steve Vandeventer, Jyotsna Garg, Audrey L Blewer, Edward Sharpe, Thomas D Rea, R Darrell Nelson, Bryan McNally, Christopher B Granger, RACE-CARS Trial Investigators
The New England journal of medicine · 2026-11-08 · doi:10.1056/nejmoa2612757
ClinicalPeer reviewedBrain–body physiologyClinical trial / evidence synthesis

Background Early cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and defibrillation improve survival after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, but evidence from randomized trials about whether community-based implementation strategies improve outcomes is lacking.

Methods We conducted a registry-based, cluster-randomized trial involving adults with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest treated by emergency medical services (EMS) in North Carolina counties. Data were collected through the Cardiac Arrest Registry to Enhance Survival.

Results From July 1, 2022, to June 30, 2026, a total of 28 clusters comprising 11,615 patients were assigned to the intervention group, and 27 clusters comprising 10,844 patients were assigned to the usual-care group.
