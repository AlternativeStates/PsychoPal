Excitation/Inhibition Balance in People at Clinical High Risk for Psychosis: A Functional MRI Study

Z. Li, I. Varvari, A. E. Cullen, G. Gifford, P. Dazzan, S. Tognin, P. Fusar-Poli, G. Modinos, R. Kahn, E. v. Hell, A. Maat, L. d. Haan, B. Crespo-Facorro, B. Glenthoj, S. Lawrie, O. Gruber, T. v. Aim Investigate excitation/inhibition-related brain dynamics, indexed by the resting-state Hurst exponent, in people at clinical high risk for psychosis.

Methods Resting-state fMRI from two longitudinal cohorts (PSYSCAN: 190 CHR-P, 115 controls; AMP SCZ: 826 CHR-P, 241 controls); Hurst exponent mapping; comparison with normative receptor distributions; transition analysis in PSYSCAN (21 transitioners).

Results CHR-P individuals had lower whole-brain Hurst exponent than controls, most prominently in somatomotor network and thalamus. Receptor distributions for noradrenaline, serotonin and opioid systems predicted the spatial pattern, and baseline regional values gave above-chance individual prediction of transition.

Conclusion Psychosis vulnerability is linked to reduced Hurst exponent, consistent with less persistent brain dynamics and increased excitability, though transition evidence is preliminary.

Why it matters for you: Connects to your interest in neurodynamics, whole-brain models and computational psychiatry, and to receptor-informed brain mapping.
