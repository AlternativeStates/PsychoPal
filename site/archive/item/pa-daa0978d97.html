Aggression experience and observation drive shared behavioural and neural changes.

Jorge M Iravedra-García, Eartha Mae Guthman, Dexter Tsin, Polina Cherepanova, Lenca Cuturela, Edgar J Ocasio-Arce, Jonathan W Pillow, Annegret L Falkner
Nature · 2026-11-04 · doi:10.1038/s41586-026-11217-9
Peer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive codingConnectivity / synchronyInformation theory / complexity

Aim Here we develop a strategy to perform large-scale cell-type-specific recordings across subcortical networks for social behaviour generation and learning.

Methods We record longitudinally while animals train through direct aggression experience or observation, then probe shared differences in behaviour and neural activity in a novel aggression context.

Results During aggression observation, observed behaviour recruits widespread activation across subcortical networks for behavioural generation and mimics activity evoked by experienced behaviour, and shared neural changes persist into the novel aggression context.

Conclusion Together, this demonstrates that experience-like activity during aggression observation can recruit a shared inhibitory plasticity mechanism that biases behaviour toward adaptive proactive behavioural strategies.
