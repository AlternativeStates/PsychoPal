Rainforest adaptation in late Homo erectus from southern China.

K E Westaway, R Joannes-Boyau, W Liao, Q Shao, W Yu, M Quinn, A-M Bacon, H Liang, N Bourgon, J-X Zhao, P Roberts, R Kono, Y Pan, L Yang, Y Wang, C Jin, Z Wu, S Murray, C Zanolli, F Demeter, F Welker, R D Ásmundsdóttir, Q Fu, V Balter, W Wang, Y Zhang
Nature · 2026-11-18 · doi:10.1038/s41586-026-11155-6
Peer reviewed

Background Homo erectus represents one of the longest-persisting hominin species, with a geographic range from Africa to eastern Eurasia from approximately 1.9 million years ago1 to 108 thousand years ago (ka)2.

Methods Here we present a rich temporal and environmental context for nine hominin teeth, eight of which were morphologically and genetically identified as H. erectus5, found in two sister caves in Guangxi province, southern China.

Results We show that H. erectus was present in southern China between 324 and 184 ka, which is substantially more recent than previously established in this region for unequivocally H. erectus fossils at approximately 400 ka.

Conclusion The evidence suggests that they were persisting in closed rainforest ecosystems, thought to be the domain of only Homo sapiens, and were demonstrating long-term mobility and ecological flexibility.
