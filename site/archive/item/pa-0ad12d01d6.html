The Association between Physical Activity and Higher Corticolimbic Synaptic Density is Independent of Body Mass Index.

Karina Moisieienko, Ruth H Asch, Mika Naganawa, Sophia D'Ambrosi, Sarah Boster, Robert H Pietrzak, Irina Esterlis
Biological psychiatry. Cognitive neuroscience and neuroimaging · 2026-11-20 · doi:10.1016/j.bpsc.2026.11.006
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#synaptic density#pet#physical activity

Aim Whether physical activity is associated with higher synaptic density in corticolimbic regions of the living human brain.

Methods PET with [18F]SynVesT-1 in 51 individuals (61% female), grouped as physically active or insufficiently active; group comparisons, regression and mediation analyses including BMI and depressive symptoms.

Results Physically active participants had higher synaptic density across corticolimbic regions (F6,44 = 2.41, p = 0.042), remaining marginal after adjusting for BMI and depressive symptoms (p = 0.050). Region-specific associations were positive, and BMI did not mediate the relationships.

Conclusion Physical activity is associated with higher synaptic density, particularly in prefrontal circuits, partly independent of BMI. Longitudinal studies are needed to establish causality.
