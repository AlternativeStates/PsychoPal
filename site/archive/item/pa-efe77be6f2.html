Novel SV2A PET/MRI reveals mechanism behind whole brain synaptic density changes during radiation therapy for treatment of brain tumors

Nazanin Maleki, Behnaz Khazai, Marta Baro, Takuya Toyonaga, Anthony Young, Sowmya Shree Gopal, Matthew Sala, Divya Sanjeevi Ramakrishnan, David Weiss, Alaaddin Ibrahimy, Evan D. Morris, Jason Cai, Veronica Chiang, Anita J Huttner, Michael Hollander, Joseph Contessa, Jason Cai, Richard Carson, Mariam Aboian
Neuro-Oncology · 2026-11-01 · doi:10.1093/neuonc/noag189.849
Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingPre-clinical / invasiveMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#sv2a pet#synaptic density#radiotherapy

Aim Identify an early imaging biomarker of synaptic change after whole-brain radiation, to predict cognitive decline in a rodent model.

Methods Seventy-two Sprague-Dawley rats underwent baseline and follow-up SV2A PET (18F-SynVesT-1) and DTI MRI after 3 Gy x 10 fractions of whole-brain, hemisphere or sham irradiation, with kinetic modelling and Western blotting.

Results Irradiated regions showed increased tracer uptake at 1 month, with hippocampal DVR of 1.5 versus 1.34 in non-irradiated tissue (p < 0.05), similar changes in frontal, insular and amygdala regions. Differences disappeared at 3 and 6 months, and R1 and diffusion measures were unchanged.

Conclusion The authors conclude that SV2A PET is an early biomarker of radiation-induced synaptic change that may predict cognitive outcome, though this rests on a rodent model.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your synaptic density PET ([11C]UCB-J/SV2A) work, here applied to radiation neurotoxicity with a different SV2A tracer.
