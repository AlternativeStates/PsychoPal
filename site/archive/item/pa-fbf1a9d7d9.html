A shared interbacterial membrane underlies mycobacterial cording.

Marlen Petersen, Richa Mishra, Miriam S Weber, Anna Rafalik, Bibek Acharya, Turner P Brown, Vera Lechner, Clément Bousquet, Melanie Hoos, Alexandra Kummer, Eugenia Guerrero Aruffo, Casey Papson, Alex Aboagye, Bei Shi Lee, Sönke Sdunnus, Michael Berney, Melanie Hannebelle, Margot Riggi, Benjamin M Swarts, Matthieu Chavent, Wonpil Im, Gregor L Weiss, Vivek V Thacker
Cell · 2026-11-24 · doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.11.002
Peer reviewed#mycobacteria#cryo-et#membranes

Aim How individual mycobacterial cells coordinate growth, division and adhesion to form serpentine cords.

Methods Live-cell fluorescence microscopy combined with cryo-electron tomography of virulent mycobacteria.

Results Snapping division generates a shared interbacterial membrane between daughter cells, extending laterally during cord formation. Lipid composition and an interleaflet density associated with virulence-factor secretion were observed in M. tuberculosis.

Conclusion A multi-bacterial membrane architecture provides the structural basis for cording and collective behaviours such as mechanical stability and phage resistance.
