Targeting IL-1 Signaling to Improve Antidepressant Efficacy in a Rodent Model of Postpartum Depression

Romina Garcia de leon, Wansu Qiu, Phoenix Au-Yeung, Stephanie Lieblich, Amanda B. Namchuk, Liisa A.M. Galea
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-26 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.26.769549
SSRIs / antidepressantsDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPreprintComputational / whole-brain modellingPre-clinical / invasive#postpartum depression#il-1#fluoxetine

Aim Test whether IL-1 receptor antagonism improves fluoxetine's effects on neuroplasticity and behaviour in a rodent model of postpartum depression.

Methods Rats given high corticosterone for 21 days postpartum, treated with fluoxetine, anakinra or both; maternal care, forced swim, hippocampal neurogenesis and perineuronal net counts in hippocampus and frontal cortex.

Results Combined treatment reversed corticosterone-induced changes in neurogenesis and perineuronal net numbers, while either drug alone did not. Behavioural passive coping in the forced swim test was not reversed.

Conclusion IL-1 signalling may limit antidepressant-induced neuroplasticity postpartum, and IL-1 receptor antagonism is a promising adjunct, though neuroplastic and behavioural effects dissociated.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your interest in neuroplasticity, inflammation and SSRI response, and to perineuronal nets as a plasticity marker.
