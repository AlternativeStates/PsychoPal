Rhodopsin 7 is an ancestral non-canonical photoreceptor that shapes light-responsive behavior.

Valentina Kirsch, Nils Reinhard, Heiko Hartlieb, Annika Mohr, Dirk Rieger, Peter Soba, Charlotte Helfrich-Förster, Pingkalai R Senthilan
eLife · 2026-11-18 · doi:10.7554/elife.109811
Peer reviewed#rhodopsin#drosophila#non-visual light

Aim Whether Rhodopsin 7, a widely expressed non-canonical photoreceptor, shapes light-responsive behaviour in Drosophila.

Methods Expression mapping, behavioural assays of activity under light and dark, constitutively active RH7 strains, and phylogenetic comparisons across arthropods.

Results RH7 detects temporal luminance changes and guides rapid responses. Flies lacking RH7 were less active in the dark phase, while constitutively active RH7 increased activity. RH7 appears ancient across panarthropods.

Conclusion RH7 acts as a melanopsin-like sensor shaping behaviour and represents an evolutionary intermediate in non-visual light perception.

Why it matters for you: Non-visual light sensing shaping daily activity patterns connects loosely to your interest in circadian and non-image-forming brain function.
