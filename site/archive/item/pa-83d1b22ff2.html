First-in-class -PET radiotracers for GPR39: A platform for noninvasive target engagement imaging in vivo

B. Bhoopal, R. W. Fitzgerald, K. Gollapelli, M. Miller, N. Damuka, C. Baity, P. Roy, P. Myburgh, D. Zhou, P. Jadiya, R. Cerevera-Juanes, K. K. Solingapuram Sai
biorxiv (neuroscience) · 2026-11-09 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.06.764940
PETDementia / Alzheimer'sPre-clinicalPreprintMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)

Background However, the lack of suitable positron emission tomography (PET) radiotracers has limited the in vivo characterization of GPR39.

Methods Quantitative assessment of GPR39 in vivo could improve our understanding of its role in physiology and disease and facilitate the development and evaluation of targeted therapeutics.

Results GPR39 specificity was further validated in GPR39 knockout mice, which showed 76% (p<0.002) and 90% (p<0.0002) reductions in brain uptake by PET imaging and biodistribution, respectively. Moreover, 5xFAD transgenic mice exhibited significantly lower brain uptake by PET imaging (40%, p<0.0002) and biodistribution (77%, p<0.002), indicating sensitivity of [18F]GPR39 2.1 to disease-associated alterations in GPR39.

Conclusion Collectively, these results establish [18F]GPR39 2.1 as a stable, brain-penetrant, and highly specific PET radiotracer for noninvasive imaging of GPR39 in vivo.
