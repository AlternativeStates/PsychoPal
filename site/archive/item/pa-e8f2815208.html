Embodied Dynamic Causal Modelling: Brain and Cardiac Effective Connectivity

A. D. Levy, P. Zeidman, M. Allen, K. Friston
biorxiv (neuroscience) · 2026-11-03 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.27.762241
PreprintConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain–body physiologyClinical trial / evidence synthesis#dynamic causal modelling#brain-heart#interoception

Aim To introduce Embodied Dynamic Causal Modelling for estimating effective connectivity across the cortex, brainstem and heart.

Methods Extension of generative causal modelling with a closed-loop brainstem cardiac neural mass model including five brainstem nuclei, a thalamic relay and the sinoatrial node, jointly inverted with simultaneous EEG and ECG during an auditory Go/No-Go task.

Results The framework distinguished granular from agranular microcircuit hypotheses within the viscero-sensorimotor hierarchy and recovered time-varying synaptic efficacy with latent neuronal dynamics across anticipation, target and inhibition windows.

Conclusion Linking neural and cardiac signals in one generative architecture moves brain-heart research beyond correlation and offers a way to test embodied inference theories.

Why it matters for you: Central to your interests in brain-heart coupling, interoception, and DCM applied to brain-body interactions.
