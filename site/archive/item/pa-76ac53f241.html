Changes in self-related cognitive processes and depressive symptom severity following a single, moderate dose of psilocybin in depressed patients.

Lydia Belinger, Flora Moujaes, Robin von Rotz, Nathalie M Rieser, Xaver Funk, Klemens Egger, Lena Meinhold, Maike Renkert, Philipp Stämpfli, Marcus Herdener, Franz X Vollenweider, Katrin H Preller
Philosophical transactions of the Royal Society of London. Series B, Biological sciences · 2026 · doi:10.1098/rstb.2025.0168
PsilocybinDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis

Background Psychedelic-assisted therapy, particularly with psilocybin, is increasingly proposed as a treatment for major depressive disorder, yet the neural and psychological mechanisms underlying its effects remain under investigation.

Methods In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial, individuals diagnosed with major depressive disorder received a single, moderate dose of psilocybin (0.215 mg kg-1) or placebo, with the same psychological support.

Results Compared with placebo, the psilocybin group exhibited significant reductions in depressive symptoms and a significant increase in positive self-concept two weeks after substance administration. Changes in self-concept were significantly correlated with decreased depressive scores.

Conclusion These findings extend prior evidence linking psychedelics to changes in self-referential processing and self-concept and demonstrate that such changes occur concurrently with reductions in depressive symptoms.
