From antidepressants to psychedelics: revisiting the pharmacology of so-called serotoninergic treatments

Marc Gielen, Daniela Duarte Domingues, Eric Burgui{è}re, Kevin Swierkosz- Lenart, Luc Mallet
arXiv (Cornell University) · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.48550/arxiv.2611.20834
Psychedelics (general)Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)OCDPreprintPre-clinical / invasive#trkb#ssri#psychedelic pharmacology

Aim Revisit the molecular mechanisms of antidepressants and OCD drugs to question whether SERT inhibition alone explains SSRI effects and psychedelic action.

Methods Narrative pharmacological review of the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of SSRIs and serotoninergic psychedelics, with focus on TrkB as a putative secondary target.

Results SSRI dosing needed for depression and OCD is consistent with a major target of much lower SSRI affinity than SERT, such as TrkB, which may offer a unifying account of antidepressant effects.

Conclusion TrkB may provide a unifying framework for antidepressant action, with implications for the therapeutic potential of psychedelics.

Why it matters for you: Offers a molecular framework for psychedelic and SSRI mechanisms that bears on your computational chemistry and psychedelic pharmacology interests.
