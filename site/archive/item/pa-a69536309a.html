Age-dependent structural brain aging in medication-free major depressive disorder: A multi-site analysis from COORDINATE-MDD consortium.

Bhanu Sharma, Pedro L Ballester, Luciano Minuzzi, Wenyi Xiao, Mathilde Antoniades, Dhivya Srinivasan, Guray Erus, Jose A Garcia, Yong Fan, Danilo Arnone, Stephen R Arnott, Taolin Chen, Ki Sueng Choi, Katharine Dunlop, Cherise Chin Fatt, Rachel D Woodham, Beata R Godlewska, Stefanie Hassel, Keith Ho, Andrew M McIntosh, Kun Qin, Susan Rotzinger, Matthew D Sacchet, Jonathan Savitz, Haochang Shou, Ashish Singh, Vibe G Frokjaer, Melanie Ganz, Aleks Stolicyn, Irina Strigo, Duygu Tosun, Dongtao Wei, Ian M Anderson, W Edward Craighead, J F William Deakin, Boadie W Dunlop, Rebecca Elliott, Qiyong Gong, Ian H Gotlib, Catherine J Harmer, Sidney H Kennedy, Gitte M Knudsen, Helen S Mayberg, Martin P Paulus, Jiang Qiu, Madhukar H Trivedi, Heather C Whalley, Chao-Gan Yan, Allan H Young, Christos Davatzikos, Cynthia H Y Fu, Benicio N Frey

Aim To test whether brain aging, measured as a brain age gap, is elevated in medication-free people with major depressive disorder and whether it depends on age and neuroanatomical phenotype.

Methods Harmonised T1-weighted MRI from 645 medication-free people with a current depressive episode and 645 matched healthy controls across 11 sites; gradient boosting brain age estimation with nested cross-validation and SHAP regional contributions.

Results Major depressive disorder was associated with a higher age-corrected brain age gap than controls (mean difference +2.0 years, Cohen's d -0.289). Gaps were absent before the mid-30s and reached +6.8 years in those aged 55 and older before site adjustment (4.3 years after).

Conclusion Structural brain aging in depression is age-dependent and strongest in a clinically vulnerable phenotype, consistent with but not establishing neuroprogression models.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your interest in neuroprogression and neurodynamics in depression, and to multi-site MRI harmonisation of clinical samples.
