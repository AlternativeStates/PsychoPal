Reference-region normalization outperforms data-driven approaches in [18F]FDG PET of dementia with Lewy bodies.

Antoine Rogeau, Fiene Marie Kuijper, Romain Delsanti, Théophile Bieth, Dario Saracino, Raffaella Migliaccio, Fabien Hauw, Marc Teichmann, Stéphanie Bombois, Nadya Pyatigorskaya, Marine Soret, Thibaud Charrier, Marie Chupin, François-Xavier Lejeune, Hugo Boniface, Nicolas Villain, Aurélie Kas
NeuroImage · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.122359
Dementia / Alzheimer'sHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#pet#fdg#normalisation

Aim To compare intensity normalisation methods for [18F]FDG PET in dementia with Lewy bodies, identifying the most suitable approach.

Methods [18F]FDG PET/MR in 110 DLB patients and 30 controls; reference-region, proportional scaling, iterative and histogram-based methods compared on normative alignment, disease independence, stability and simulated phantom recovery.

Results Reference-region normalisation gave smaller DLB-control scaling differences, weaker meta-ROI correlations and lower variability than data-driven methods. The white matter and pons had the highest composite scores.

Conclusion Reference-region normalisation is preferred in DLB, outperforming proportional scaling, histogram and data-driven approaches in this large cohort.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your [18F]FDG and PET quantification work, offering a benchmark for normalisation choices in your own protocols.
