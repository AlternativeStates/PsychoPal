Roots to Thrive risk and readiness assessment: a practice-based intake and decision-support framework for psychedelic-assisted therapy.

Shannon S Dames, Vivian W L Tsang, Julia Sheffield, Barbara Fehlau, Sydney McLachlan, Pamela Kryskow
BMJ open quality · 2026-11-13 · doi:10.1136/bmjoq-2026-004275
PsilocybinKetamine / esketamineSubstance use (other)Peer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#psychedelic-assisted therapy#clinical governance#intake

Aim To develop and describe a trauma-informed readiness and risk assessment framework for community-based psychedelic-assisted therapy intake.

Methods Multi-year practice-based quality improvement process from 2020 within a community psychedelic-assisted therapy programme and a university graduate setting, using iterative clinical application, consultation and feedback.

Results The framework integrates biomedical, psychological, relational, cultural, ecological and transpersonal domains to support contextual intake decisions, with reported gains in shared language and alignment with community practice.

Conclusion The framework shifts screening from exclusion towards readiness and risk matched to supports, though it is not yet psychometrically validated and was developed in one setting.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to the translation and governance side of psychedelic therapy, a practice-based view that sits beside your clinical pharmacology interests.
