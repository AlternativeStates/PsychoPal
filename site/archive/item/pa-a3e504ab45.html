Animal models in psychedelic research - Tripping over translation.

Muad Y Abd El Hay, Ana Cukić, Marieke L Schölvinck, Martha N Havenith
Translational psychiatry · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04589-z
Psychedelics (general)Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)PTSDAnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingClinical trial / evidence synthesisPre-clinical / invasive#psychedelics#animal models#translation

Aim To audit how rodent psychedelic studies are designed and whether their conditions and assays could translate to human therapy.

Methods Systematic review of 266 rodent psychedelic studies published 2014 to 2026, assessing welfare and set-and-setting-like factors, behavioural assay design, and individual and social factors.

Results Most studies included stress-inducing factors: only 14% tested in the active phase, 7% used environmental enrichment, and 21% used refined handling. Drug administration almost universally used restraint-requiring methods. Most assays were brief and isolated, and group-level cross-sectional designs dominated.

Conclusion The authors argue translation requires longitudinal tracking of individuals in enriched social settings with comprehensive behavioural characterisation.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your psychedelic clinical pharmacology and preclinical translation questions, including how set and setting shape human outcomes.
