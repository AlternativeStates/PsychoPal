Ketamine-Augmented Hypnotherapy.

Dirk Revenstorf, Joost Mertens, Martin Schipplick
Brain sciences · 2026-10-27 · doi:10.3390/brainsci16111166
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesisPre-clinical / invasive#ketamine#hypnotherapy#depression

Aim To describe ketamine-augmented hypnotherapy (KAHT) and report preliminary feasibility and outcome data from an uncontrolled case series.

Methods Narrative review of psychedelic, ketamine-induced and hypnotic states, followed by a description of KAHT: a single 0.5 mg/kg racemic ketamine infusion under hypnotherapeutic guidance, with preliminary data from 89 patients including MEQ and pre-post PHQ-9 in 20.

Results Most patients described calming or psychedelic effects, with highest MEQ scores for transcendence of time and space. PHQ-9 fell significantly (p = 0.048), though not significantly in the clinically depressed subgroup (p = 0.09). Of 20 patients, 12 reported lasting benefit.

Conclusion KAHT appears feasible across diagnoses but responses vary; the uncontrolled design cannot isolate the hypnotherapy contribution, so randomised trials are needed.

Why it matters for you: Ketamine-assisted psychotherapy with a clear uncontrolled design is a useful reference point for planning the controlled psychedelic trials you care about.
