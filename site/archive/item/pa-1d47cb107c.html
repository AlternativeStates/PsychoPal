Effects of 5-HT2A Receptor Activation on Oxycodone Demand after Short and Long Access Self-Administration in Male and Female Rats.

Carter J Richardson, Alice J McQueney, Thomas Stevens, Erik J Garcia
Psychedelic medicine (New Rochelle, N.Y.) · 2026-01-07 · doi:10.1177/28314425251408808
Psychedelics (general)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewed#5-HT2A#psychedelics#addiction

Aim Whether 5-HT2A receptor activation by DOI reduces oxycodone demand after short or long access self-administration in male and female rats.

Methods Male and female Sprague-Dawley rats with jugular catheters self-administered oxycodone (0.1 mg/kg/infusion) under short or long access; DOI, volinanserin and SB242084 were tested using a threshold demand procedure with exponentiated demand analysis.

Results DOI dose-dependently reduced oxycodone demand in short-access rats in both sexes. After long access, low DOI doses reduced demand while the highest doses enhanced reinforcement; most effects were blocked by volinanserin, which itself reduced demand in long-access rats.

Conclusion Hallucinogenic 5-HT2A activation reduces oxycodone demand in a sex- and access-dependent way, and nonselective phenethylamines may prove less useful than selective agents.

Why it matters for you: Extends 5-HT2AR pharmacology relevant to psychedelic clinical pharmacology, though it is preclinical and focused on addiction rather than your core neuroimaging work.
