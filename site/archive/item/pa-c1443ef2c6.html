Working memory neural correlates after ketamine in treatment-resistant depression: a randomized placebo-controlled functional magnetic resonance imaging study.

Adam Fijtman, Níall Lally, Jennifer Evans, Gregory H Jones, Jonathan P Roiser, Carlos A Zarate
Psychiatry research. Neuroimaging · 2026-11-03 · doi:10.1016/j.pscychresns.2026.112367
Ketamine / esketaminefMRIDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#ketamine#treatment-resistant depression#fmri

Aim To examine working memory behaviour and fMRI activation after ketamine in treatment-resistant depression versus healthy volunteers.

Methods Secondary analysis of a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in 22 unmedicated treatment-resistant depression participants and 19 healthy volunteers, given ketamine (0.5 mg/kg) or placebo with 3T fMRI during an N-back task.

Results Ketamine significantly reduced depression scores versus placebo (p < 0.01), but N-back performance did not differ by drug condition. Region-specific activation changes were observed, including reduced parieto-occipital junction activation after ketamine.

Conclusion Ketamine's antidepressant effect occurred without significant working memory change, though task-related regional changes were seen. Modest sample size and exploratory ROI analyses limit interpretation.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your ketamine neuroimaging work and EEG and fMRI methods, though this is a modest exploratory analysis rather than a plasticity study.
