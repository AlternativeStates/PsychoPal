Connectome modulates critical brain dynamics across local and global scales

Giovanni Rabuffo, Pietro Bozzo, Bach Nguyen, Damien Depannemaecker, Marco N. Pompili, Leonardo L. Gollo, Tomoki Fukai, Pierpaolo Sorrentino, Leonardo Dalla Porta
Physical Review Research · 2026-11-17 · doi:10.1103/n87d-5krk
fMRIPre-clinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyInformation theory / complexityComputational / whole-brain modelling#criticality#whole-brain model#neural mass

Aim How local near-criticality within brain regions interacts with global criticality when regions are coupled through a mouse connectome.

Methods Multiscale model with neural mass models tuned near the asynchronous-synchronous transition, coupled via an empirical mouse connectome, plus whole-brain simulations of connectome-coupled spiking neurons.

Results Local criticality was modulated by long-range coupling, producing nonlinear timescale gradients. Global criticality, with scale-free avalanches and optimal functional connectivity, emerged only when local populations were near criticality and coupled within an optimal range.

Conclusion Local and global criticality are dynamically intertwined but not directly aligned, and their relationship depends on structural connectivity.
