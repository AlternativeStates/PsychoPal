Chronic intracranial neural dynamics forecast treatment response and therapeutic engagement during deep brain stimulation for OCD

Nicole Provenza, Eric A Storch, Wayne K. Goodman, Sameer Sheth
USC Mark and Mary Stevens Neuroimaging and Informatics Institute · 2026-11-10 · doi:10.18120/kt39-zq59
OCDClinicalPeer reviewedBrain stimulationPre-clinical / invasive

Background Clinical improvement following psychiatric neuromodulation often requires weeks to months, creating prolonged uncertainty during treatment optimization.

Methods We analyzed nearly 120,000 patient-hours of chronic ventral striatal recordings from 24 patients undergoing deep brain stimulation for treatment-resistant obsessive-compulsive disorder.

Results Clinical responders exhibited rapid reductions in neural predictability within two weeks of stimulation, months before they achieved clinical response. The magnitude of this early neural change correlated significantly with eventual symptom reduction.

Conclusion Together, these findings establish a framework for leveraging chronic neural sensing to inform therapeutic target engagement and longitudinal monitoring of treatment response.
