Cannabinoid receptor type 1 availability in individuals with a history of childhood trauma: a positron emission tomography study.

Ardavan Mohammad Aghaei, Brian Pittman, Nachshon Korem, Marc N Potenza, Deepak Cyril D'Souza, Ansel T Hillmer, Mohini Ranganathan, Ilan Harpaz-Rotem, Anahita Bassir Nia
Molecular psychiatry · 2026-10-31 · doi:10.1038/s41380-026-03931-7
PETDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)PTSDHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#cb1 receptor#childhood trauma#pet

Aim Compare CB1 receptor availability in adults with childhood trauma against healthy controls using PET.

Methods Twenty-two adults with childhood trauma and 22 age- and sex-matched healthy controls underwent [11C]OMAR PET, with linear models for global, amygdala, hippocampal and frontal regions.

Results Lower CB1R availability was seen globally (-11.36%, p=0.04), in amygdala (-13.70%, p=0.01) and hippocampus (-14.50%, p=0.01), not frontal cortex. PTSD, MDD and medication did not show effects.

Conclusion The authors conclude this preliminary result suggests endocannabinoid dysregulation linked to childhood trauma, requiring replication.

Why it matters for you: Connects to your interest in the endocannabinoid and PET receptor imaging, extended to trauma in humans.
