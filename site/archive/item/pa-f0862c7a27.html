Increased signal diversity/complexity of spontaneous EEG, but not evoked EEG responses, in ketamine-induced psychedelic state in humans

Nadine Farnes, Bjørn Erik Juel, André Sevenius Nilsen, Luis Georg Romundstad, Johan Frederik Storm
PLoS ONE · 2020-11-23 · doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0242056
Ketamine / esketamineEEGTMS / TMS-EEGHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive codingConnectivity / synchronyInformation theory / complexityBrain stimulation#ketamine#eeg complexity#tms-eeg

Aim Whether EEG signal diversity, measured spontaneously and evoked by TMS, changes under sub-anaesthetic ketamine and relates to subjective experience.

Methods Open-label within-subject study in 10 healthy volunteers with 62-channel EEG, recording TMS-evoked responses (PCI) and spontaneous EEG (Lempel-Ziv complexity, amplitude and synchrony coalition entropy) before and during sub-anaesthetic ketamine.

Results No significant change in evoked complexity (PCI). All spontaneous signal diversity measures increased under ketamine, correlating with subjective altered states, and were higher with eyes open than closed.

Conclusion Spontaneous and evoked complexity may reflect complementary aspects; ketamine may raise conscious-content complexity while leaving overall capacity for consciousness largely unchanged.
