Connectome Harmonic Analysis at 7 Tesla Reveals Selective Reorganization of Large-Scale Cortical Dynamics in Adult Users of Cannabis.

Alan Francis, Francesca Filbey, Ihsan M. Salloum
Research Square · 2026-11-20 · doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-11021187/v1
fMRISubstance use (other)Psychosis / schizophreniaClinicalPreprintSpectral / aperiodic / waveformConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling#cannabis#connectome harmonics#fmri

Aim Whether cannabis use alters large-scale brain organisation, tested as changes in low-frequency connectome harmonics using 7T resting-state fMRI.

Methods Human Connectome Project 7T data from 81 cannabis users and 81 matched non-users; BOLD activity projected onto the first 200 connectome harmonics; t-tests with FDR correction and atlas localisation.

Results Cannabis users showed significantly greater power in one low-frequency harmonic (k = 6; t ≈ 4.19; p_FDR = 7.21 × 10⁻³; d = 0.659), roughly 44% higher than non-users, localised to a frontoparietal–sensorimotor network. It did not correlate with cannabis exposure variables, and behavioural associations did not survive FDR correction.

Conclusion Cannabis use is associated with selective amplification of one frontoparietal–sensorimotor eigenmode. The authors note the behavioural findings are nominal only.

Why it matters for you: Connectome harmonics offer a macroscale eigenmode lens that complements your work on neurodynamics and whole-brain models.
