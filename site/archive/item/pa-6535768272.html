Interoceptive aberrations in major depressive disorder: Neural mechanisms, clinical correlates, and targeted interventions

Hui Deng, Ya-Qing Wang, Yi-Fan Sun, Zhen-He Zhou, Zhen-He Zhou
World Journal of Psychiatry · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.5498/wjp.124324
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Peer reviewedConnectivity / synchrony#interoception#major depression#active inference

Aim How interoceptive accuracy, sensibility and awareness are altered in major depressive disorder, and which neural mechanisms and interventions are involved.

Methods Narrative review comparing interoception assessment methods and synthesising behavioural, neurobiological and clinical findings, interpreted through an active-inference framework.

Results Interoceptive dysfunction in MDD is multidimensional, varying across task performance, bodily appraisal, metacognitive calibration and neural processing. Default mode network activity differs by illness stage, lower in early-stage and higher in chronic-stage samples.

Conclusion Measurement differences and illness stage likely explain inconsistent findings, and selected interoceptive processes may be modifiable, supporting mechanism-based testing in MDD.

Why it matters for you: Central to your interest in brain–body coupling and interoception, applied to depression, which is your clinical focus.
