Environmental enrichment during adolescence ameliorates anxiety-related corticosterone, enhances memory, and is associated with changes in regional dopamine levels in the Wistar Kyoto rat, a putative model of endogenous depression.

Sujay Daniel, Chennabasava Allalli, Monika Sadananda
Cognitive, affective & behavioral neuroscience · 2026-11-09 · doi:10.3758/s13415-026-01512-3
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)AnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling#environmental enrichment#adolescence#dopamine

Aim To assess whether environmental enrichment during adolescence reduces anxiety, enhances memory and alters monoamine levels in Wistar-Kyoto rats, a model of endogenous depression.

Methods Male and female Wistar-Kyoto rats exposed to enrichment from postnatal day 22 and tested in mid, late adolescence or young adulthood using novel object recognition, elevated plus maze and radial arm maze, with ELISA and LC-MS/MS for monoamines, BDNF and corticosterone.

Results Enrichment enhanced recognition memory in adolescents and spatial memory in young adults, and reduced serum corticosterone. It increased dopamine in several brain regions at mid and late adolescence, with sex-specific differences.

Conclusion Enrichment may enhance cognition and reduce anxiety with dopamine changes, suggesting therapeutic potential for stress-related disorders in adolescents.
