Cell-type specific modulation of dorsal raphe serotonergic neurons by ketamine.

C Hmaied, S Oubraim, S Koulchitsky, I Gladwyn-Ng, S Haj-Dahmane, V Seutin
iScience · 2026-11-05 · doi:10.1016/j.isci.2026.118121
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedPre-clinical / invasive#ketamine#dorsal raphe#bdnf

Aim How ketamine modulates dorsal raphe serotonergic neurons and their excitatory inputs in a projection-specific way.

Methods Ex vivo electrophysiology and retrograde circuit mapping in rodents, with TrkB receptor blockade to test BDNF-dependent signalling.

Results Ketamine increased DRN serotonergic firing and potentiated glutamatergic synapses projecting to the medial prefrontal cortex while depressing those to the central amygdala. Synaptic potentiation was abolished by TrkB blockade.

Conclusion The authors identify parallel TrkB-dependent and TrkB-independent pathways through which ketamine regulates DRN serotonergic neuron function.

Why it matters for you: Strongly relevant to your ketamine neuroplasticity and serotonin circuit work, particularly the BDNF-TrkB mechanism.
