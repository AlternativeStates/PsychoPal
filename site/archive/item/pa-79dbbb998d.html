A staged microstructural mechanism of electroconvulsive therapy: disruption, potentiation, and rewiring.

Akihiro Takamiya, Frank Riemer, Olga Therese Ousdal, Hauke Bartsch, Ketil Joachim Oedegaard, Ute Kessler, Leif Oltedal
Molecular psychiatry · 2026-11-26 · doi:10.1038/s41380-026-03975-9
MRI (structural/diffusion)Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modellingMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#ect#diffusion mri#neuroplasticity

Aim Whether electroconvulsive therapy works through a staged process of disruption followed by neuroplastic potentiation and network rewiring, tested with longitudinal multishell diffusion MRI.

Methods Densely sampled longitudinal multishell diffusion MRI in 22 people with depression receiving ECT and 15 matched healthy controls, with scans before and after the first session, 7-14 days after the last session, and at 6 months; 141 scans analysed.

Results ECT increased hindered diffusion and decreased free water, mainly in right-lateralised grey matter, consistent with neuroplastic remodelling rather than oedema. Clinical improvement was linked to increased white matter restricted diffusion, most strongly between the second and third time points.

Conclusion The findings give in vivo human evidence for a staged disruption-rewiring process underlying ECT, a framework for how perturbation may become therapeutic reorganisation.

Why it matters for you: Diffusion microstructure and staged plasticity after a neuromodulatory treatment speak directly to your interest in neuroplasticity markers across modalities.
