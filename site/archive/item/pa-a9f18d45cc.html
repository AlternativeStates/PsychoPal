A shared meaning component across languages in the human brain.

Andrea Gregor de Varda, Saima Malik-Moraleda, Greta Tuckute, Evelina Fedorenko
Nature neuroscience · 2026-11-23 · doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02523-6
Peer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling

Background Human languages differ profoundly in their sounds, words and grammatical systems, yet all support the communication of complex meanings.

Methods Leveraging these advances, we evaluated the similarity of linguistic representations in speakers of 21 languages.

Results Model representations predicted brain responses reliably within each language. Critically, encoding models trained on one set of languages generalized zero-shot to previously unseen languages, accurately predicting neural responses without exposure to the target language.

Conclusion These findings point to a shared cross-lingual component for meaning across human languages.
