Abnormalities and plasticity of white matter functional networks in opioid use disorder: a BOLD fMRI study.

Wenhan Yang, Yifan Li, Xinwen Wen, Longtao Yang, Jun Zhang, Kai Yuan, Jun Liu
Psychological medicine · 2026-11-03 · doi:10.1017/s0033291726106035
Opioid use disorderHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchrony#white matter#opioid use disorder#fmri

Aim To investigate architecture and activity of white matter functional networks in opioid use disorder and their change after prolonged abstinence.

Methods BOLD fMRI white matter signals analysed with k-means clustering in individuals with OUD at baseline and follow-up after prolonged abstinence, compared with healthy controls.

Results At baseline the deep frontoparietal network fragmented into the superior longitudinal fasciculus and anterior corona radiata networks, partially recovering after abstinence. Hyperactivation in the deep frontal network and hypoactivation in the frontotemporal-parietal network were present, with the latter negatively correlated with years of opioid use.

Conclusion OUD disrupts white matter network architecture and activity, while prolonged abstinence promotes partial restoration, indicating neuroplastic potential of white matter networks.
