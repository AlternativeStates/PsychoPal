Altered directional brain-heart interactions across resting states and frequency bands in schizophrenia.

Xiaoyan Peng, Ling Zhao, Ying Cheng, Yanzhao Zhang, Bailing Shen, Huaihong A, Yongguang Wang
Behavioural brain research · 2026-11-14 · doi:10.1016/j.bbr.2026.116575
EEGPsychosis / schizophreniaHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain–body physiology#brain-heart interaction#schizophrenia#eeg

Aim Whether directional brain-heart interactions differ between patients with schizophrenia and healthy men across resting states and EEG frequency bands.

Methods Simultaneous resting-state EEG and ECG in 33 medicated men with schizophrenia and 29 healthy men, eyes open and eyes closed; directional brain-heart interaction across two coupling directions, two cardiac components and five EEG bands, with permutation and sensitivity analyses.

Results Patients showed higher theta, alpha and beta EEG power at eyes-open rest and lower global brain-to-heart high-frequency coupling across all five bands at eyes-open rest. Heart-to-brain effects did not survive model order sensitivity analysis, and no condition-by-group interaction was found.

Conclusion Schizophrenia is associated with reduced eyes-open brain-to-heart high-frequency coupling across EEG bands, indicating altered statistical brain-heart coupling.
