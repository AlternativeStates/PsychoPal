Short-term synaptic plasticity modulates the outcome of neurodegenerative diseases.

Han Zhang, Yusuf Mohideen, Chi Chung Alan Fung
Frontiers in computational neuroscience · 2026-10-23 · doi:10.3389/fncom.2026.1923602
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Dementia / Alzheimer'sPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modellingPre-clinical / invasive

Background Alzheimer's disease (AD) is characterized by progressive neuronal loss and synaptic dysfunction that disrupt memory-encoding neural circuits.

Methods Using the NEST Simulator, a spiking neural network of 1,000 Hodgkin-Huxley excitatory neurons and 250 leaky integrate-and-fire inhibitory neurons was constructed with embedded memory engram assemblies.

Results Increasing τfac from 500 ms to 1,250ms rescued engram selectivity in the AD model at intermediate synaptic-weight regimes that were inaccessible at baseline, and partially restored theta-band LFP power while fully restoring alpha-band LFP power.

Conclusion Short-term synaptic facilitation (STF) and short-term synaptic depression (STD) modulation exert mechanistically distinct compensatory effects.
