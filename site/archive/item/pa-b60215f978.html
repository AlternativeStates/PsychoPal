A signaling signature distinguishes hallucinogenic from non-hallucinogenic psychedelics.

Sandra M Martin-Guerrero, Marco Taddei-Tardon, Jessica L Maltman, Sritanvi Potukanuma, Javier González-Maeso, Pedro R Cutillas, Juan F Lopez-Gimenez
Translational psychiatry · 2026-09-01 · doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04390-y
Non-hallucinogenic analoguesAnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedPre-clinical / invasive#psychedelics#phosphoproteomics#5-ht2a

Aim Determine whether hallucinogenic serotonergic psychedelics differ from non-hallucinogenic analogues at the level of intracellular phosphorylation signalling.

Methods Phosphoproteomic profiling of neural cells treated with chemically diverse serotonergic psychedelics and close non-hallucinogenic analogues, with follow-up of FOXK2 phosphorylation and lactate measurements.

Results Psychedelics produced coordinated phosphorylation changes across many proteins, with a signature separating hallucinogenic from non-hallucinogenic compounds. Hallucinogenic compounds, but not analogues, increased lactate accumulation.

Conclusion Under defined in vitro conditions, hallucinogenic and non-hallucinogenic compounds engage separable signalling architectures; the dataset is offered as a resource.

Why it matters for you: Bears on the hallucinogenic versus non-hallucinogenic distinction you study in psychedelic pharmacology and tryptamine chemistry.
