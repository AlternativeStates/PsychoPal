Deep learning to overcome human error and bias in electrode position extraction in tDCS-fMRI studies

Filip Niemann, Steffen Riemann, Sophie Dabelstein, Kira Hering, Harun Kocataş, Mohamed Abdelmotaleb, Leonardo M. Caisachana Guevara, Rico Fischer, Axel Thielscher, Daria Antonenko, Agnes Flöel, Marcus Meinzer
Brain Informatics · 2026-11-25 · doi:10.1186/s40708-026-00335-9
MRI (structural/diffusion)ClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyBrain stimulationPreprocessing / source / toolsMachine learning / AI#tdcs#electrode localisation#deep learning

Aim To determine whether a 3D U-Net can automatically extract tDCS electrode positions from PETRA MRI scans, removing human error and bias.

Methods Five U-Net variants benchmarked with five-fold cross-validation on 54 expert-segmented images and across 4,234 images from a multi-site tDCS-fMRI study; automated, semi-automatic and manual extraction of a 3×1 montage compared against expert ground truth.

Results Ungated networks recovered all four electrodes in 97.7% and 96.3% of images, versus 89.0 to 91.2% with attention gates. Automated methods agreed closely (ICC = 0.984, bias 1.3 mm), while manual extraction showed a 6.3 mm systematic offset.

Conclusion The authors release the standard U-Net as open source, arguing automation removes a measurable error source and scales to large datasets.

Why it matters for you: Accurate electrode localisation matters for the focal stimulation and dose modelling you may run alongside neuromodulation studies.
