Testing the impact of cannabinoid stimulation on fatty acid amide hydrolase binding in the human brain: pilot [11C]CURB PET study

Raesham Mahmood, Shahtaj S. Dheda, Claudia Poluga, Tina McCluskey, Hsiang-Yuan Lin, Matthew E. Sloan, Rachel Fynvola Tyndale, Lucas Narciso, Jerry J. Warsh, Tony Peter George, Bernard Le Foll, Stefan M. Kloiber, Isabelle Boileau
Journal of Cannabis Research · 2026-11-09 · doi:10.1186/s42238-026-00531-8
Substance use (other)Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain stimulationMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#faah#cannabinoids#pet

Aim Whether repeated administration of the cannabinoid agonist nabilone changes brain FAAH availability measured with [11C]CURB PET.

Methods Exploratory Phase IV pilot with ten healthy adults, each scanned at baseline and after a 7-day nabilone regimen titrated to 2 mg/day; FAAH quantified with a two-tissue irreversible model and analysed with repeated-measures ANCOVA including genotype.

Results Nabilone increased marijuana-like effects (p = 0.004) and reduced energy (p = 0.02) and clear-headedness (p = 0.01). No detectable mean change in FAAH across brain regions was observed (p = 0.357).

Conclusion The authors conclude that no detectable change in brain FAAH followed seven days of nabilone, a preliminary finding needing confirmation in larger controlled studies.

Why it matters for you: A PET study with a novel tracer and a repeated-scan pharmacological design that closely matches your PET kinetic modelling and clinical pharmacology work.
