Bergenin ameliorates anxiety- and depression-like behavior in antibiotic-treated mice: behavioral and in silico insights.

Md Shadin, Asraful Islam Rakib, Junayet Zaman Tahmid, Sheikh Amir Hosen, Tarek Aziz, Asmaul Husna Bristy, Muhammad Torequl Islam, Mushtaq Ahmad Ansari
Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's archives of pharmacology · 2026-11-20 · doi:10.1007/s00210-026-06120-z
SSRIs / antidepressantsDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)AnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedPre-clinical / invasive#gut-brain axis#serotonin transporter#animal behaviour

Aim Whether bergenin, an anti-inflammatory isocoumarin, reverses anxiety- and depression-like behaviour induced by antibiotic-driven gut dysbiosis in mice.

Methods Antibiotic regimen in Swiss albino mice followed by 14 days of bergenin (25 or 50 mg/kg/day) or fluoxetine; elevated plus maze, open field, forced swim and tail suspension tests; docking and 100 ns molecular dynamics against TLR4-MD-2 and SERT.

Results Bergenin dose-dependently attenuated anxiety- and depression-like deficits, with high-dose recovery of about 70-92%, without altering behaviour in antibiotic-free animals. In silico, it bound the SERT S1 site with stability resembling fluoxetine, but its TLR4-MD-2 interaction was shallow.

Conclusion Bergenin ameliorates behavioural deficits in antibiotic-treated mice, and in silico data favour serotonergic over TLR4 involvement. The authors stress that the work is hypothesis-generating and that microbiome and mechanistic assays were not performed.

Why it matters for you: Gut-brain and serotonergic mechanisms in mood fit your interest in brain-body coupling, though the link is indirect.
