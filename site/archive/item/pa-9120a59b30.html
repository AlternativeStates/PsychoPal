Domain-level symptom improvement after ketamine and TMS-based stimulation in treatment-resistant depression: a post hoc pooled analysis.

Kuan-I Liu, Tung-Ping Su, Chih-Ming Cheng, Wei-Chen Lin, Ya-Mei Bai, Shih-Jen Tsai, Cheng-Ta Li, Mu-Hong Chen
Psychiatry research · 2026-11-07 · doi:10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117571
Ketamine / esketamineTMS / TMS-EEGDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)AnxietyClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain stimulationClinical trial / evidence synthesis

Background We examined short-term symptom-domain improvement after ketamine and transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) in a post hoc pooled analysis of 159 active-treatment participants from three trials.

Methods Participants received a single ketamine infusion (n = 89) or a two-week TMS course (n = 70); nine had recorded bipolar diagnoses.

Results Mean improvement differed across domains within both modalities (both Holm-adjusted p < 0.001). Main-model between-modality mean contrasts did not meet the adjusted threshold (p = 0.063-0.656); the Somatic contrast did so after adjustment for treatment-resistance categories.

Conclusion These findings describe domain-specific improvement under different treatment regimens.
