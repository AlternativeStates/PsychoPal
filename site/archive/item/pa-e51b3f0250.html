Dissociating Early Heartbeat-Evoked Afferent Gating From Late Associative Failure in Post-Anoxic Coma

Calixto Machado, José Jesús Sánchez, Arthur Schiff³, Beata Drobná Sániová⁴, M Drobný, Raed Mualem
Research Square · 2026-11-01 · doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-11341826/v1
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Disorders of consciousnessClinicalPreprintERP / MMN / predictive codingConnectivity / synchronyBrain–body physiologyPreprocessing / source / tools#heartbeat-evoked response#coma#eeg

Aim Whether the heartbeat-evoked response can separate viable from non-viable cortical function in comatose patients after cardiac arrest.

Methods Multicentre EEG and ECG from 150 comatose post-cardiac arrest patients (discovery n = 100, validation n = 50); sLORETA source localisation and Hilbert-Huang transform with empirical mode decomposition of early (150-300 ms) and late (350-500 ms) post-R-peak windows.

Results Good outcome was linked to a preserved frontocentral deflection near 200 ms localised to the anterior cingulate and anterior insula, with high instantaneous energy and phase alignment. Non-survivors showed a positive shift, energy collapse and phase desynchronisation; late windows showed diffuse dissipation in both groups.

Conclusion Preserved early interoceptive afferent gating to the cingulate and insula, rather than late associative activity, is proposed as an electrophysiological biomarker of viable cortical microcircuitry.

Why it matters for you: Heartbeat-evoked responses in clinical coma are a direct application of the interoceptive EEG methods you use, though the preprint has not been peer reviewed.
