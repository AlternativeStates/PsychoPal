Psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression: fMRI-measured brain mechanisms

Robin Lester Carhart-Harris, Leor Roseman, Mark Bolstridge, Lysia Demetriou, Justine Nienke Pannekoek, Matthew B. Wall, Mark Tanner, Mendel Kaelen, John McGonigle, Kevin Murphy, Robert Leech, H. Valerie Curran, David Nutt
Scientific Reports · 2017-10-09 · doi:10.1038/s41598-017-13282-7
PsilocybinfMRIDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling

Aim Here, cerebral blood flow (CBF) and blood oxygen-level dependent (BOLD) resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC) were measured with functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) before and after treatment with psilocybin (serotonin agonist) for treatment-resistant …

Methods Quality pre and post treatment fMRI data were collected from 16 of 19 patients.

Results Decreased depressive symptoms were observed in all 19 patients at 1-week post-treatment and 47% met criteria for response at 5 weeks. Whole-brain analyses revealed post-treatment decreases in CBF in the temporal cortex, including the amygdala.

Conclusion These data fill an important knowledge gap regarding the post-treatment brain effects of psilocybin, and are the first in depressed patients.
