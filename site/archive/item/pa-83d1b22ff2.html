First-in-class -PET radiotracers for GPR39: A platform for noninvasive target engagement imaging in vivo

B. Bhoopal, R. W. Fitzgerald, K. Gollapelli, M. Miller, N. Damuka, C. Baity, P. Roy, P. Myburgh, D. Zhou, P. Jadiya, R. Cerevera-Juanes, K. K. Solingapuram Sai
biorxiv (neuroscience) · 2026-11-09 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.06.764940
PETDementia / Alzheimer'sPre-clinicalPreprintMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#pet radiotracer#gpr39#target engagement

Aim Whether the fluorine-18 PET radiotracer [18F]GPR39 2.1 can specifically image the zinc-sensing receptor GPR39 in vivo.

Methods Preclinical study in cell uptake assays, rats with pharmacological blockade, GPR39 knockout mice, 5xFAD transgenic mice, PET imaging, biodistribution and autoradiography.

Results Brain uptake fell by up to about 90% under blockade in rats, by 76% by PET and 90% by biodistribution in knockout mice, and was reduced in 5xFAD mice, with good stability and brain penetration.

Conclusion The authors conclude [18F]GPR39 2.1 is a stable, brain-penetrant and highly specific tracer for noninvasive GPR39 imaging, supporting its use as an imaging and therapeutic target.

Why it matters for you: A new PET tracer with blockade and knockout validation, relevant to your PET radiotracer and target engagement methods.
