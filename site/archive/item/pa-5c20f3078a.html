Integrating Transformative and Existential Strategies into Trauma-Focused Psychotherapy: A Multi-Study Analysis of EMDR, 3MDR, and Psychedelic-Assisted Group Therapy

Parisa Saghafi
ERA: Education and Research Archive (University of Alberta) · 2026-11-17 · doi:10.7939/86229
Psychedelics (general)Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)PTSDAnxietyClinicalPeer reviewed#emdr#3mdr#existential therapy

Aim To test whether transformation strategies within trauma therapies help patients reach deeper existential aspects of suffering, and how interpersonal interaction and transformation contribute to accessing deeper pain and meaning.

Methods Thesis combining a secondary qualitative analysis of 18 psychiatric outpatients with suicidal ideation treated with 12 sessions of web-based EMDR, and a mixed-methods single case study of 3MDR for PTSD in a person with dissociative identity disorder, with follow-up interviews.

Results Three existential themes emerged in the EMDR cohort: self-against-self, the anguish of being, and existential despair. In the 3MDR case, PTSD, anxiety and depression scores improved, with reported resolution of existential themes and post-traumatic growth.

Conclusion The authors suggest many people with chronic suicidality face existential crises rarely addressed in manualised therapies, and that adapted, transformative approaches may be needed. Findings come from a small sample and a single case.

Why it matters for you: Links to your interest in psychedelic-assisted therapy and existential dimensions of treatment, though it does not use neuroimaging or pharmacology.
