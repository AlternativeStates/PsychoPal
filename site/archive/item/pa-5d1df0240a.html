PROPOFOL VERSUS WITH PROPOFOL-KETAMINE MIXTURE AS ANAESTHETIC AGENT FOR ELECTROCONVULSIVE THERAPY: A RANDOMIZED CONTROLLED TRIAL.

O A Koleosho-Ademua, A A Sanusi, M T Sonaike, V Lasebikan
Annals of Ibadan postgraduate medicine · 2026
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#ketamine#ect#anaesthesia

Aim Whether adding ketamine to propofol as anaesthesia for electroconvulsive therapy improves depression and cognitive outcomes compared with propofol alone.

Methods Randomised controlled trial of 70 patients aged 18-65 with DSM-5 major depressive disorder receiving ECT, allocated to propofol 0.6 mg/kg plus ketamine 0.6 mg/kg or propofol 1.2 mg/kg; HAM-D and MMSE assessed before, after the first and after the third ECT course …

Results HAM-D scores were significantly lower after the third ECT in the ketamine-propofol group (F(1,58) = 23.24, p < 0.001), but no difference after the full ECT course. MMSE scores did not differ between groups at any time point.

Conclusion Ketamine-propofol anaesthesia is associated with temporary improvement in depressive symptoms, with no cognitive advantage over propofol at any stage of treatment.

Why it matters for you: Tests ketamine as an adjunct in an ECT setting, relevant to your ketamine and plasticity research, though the effect appears temporary.
