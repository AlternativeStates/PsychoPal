Immune alterations in recent-onset psychosis: a combined analysis using cerebrospinal fluid samples and [18F]DPA-714 imaging.

Yuya Mizuno, Richard Carr, Toby Pillinger, Ines Carreira Figueiredo, Guy Hindley, Sameer Jauhar, Thomas Whitehurst, Henry Allberry, Josep Macmillan, Oliver Hargrove, Giulia Gambino, Sami Jeljeli, Joel T Dunn, Alexander Hammers, Ramla Awais, Kerstin Sander, Erik Årstad, Julia J Schubert, Mattia Veronese, Federico E Turkheimer, Valeria Mondelli, Melanie S Hart, Andrew J Church, Tiago Reis Marques, Oliver D Howes
Brain, behavior, and immunity · 2026-11-23 · doi:10.1016/j.bbi.2026.107288
Psychosis / schizophreniaHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#neuroinflammation#tspo pet#psychosis

Aim To test whether cerebrospinal fluid cytokine levels and TSPO PET signal indicate immune dysregulation in recent-onset psychosis.

Methods Case-control study of 43 patients with recent-onset schizophrenia or related psychoses and 25 healthy controls, with lumbar puncture cytokine panels and [18F]DPA-714 TSPO PET in 84% of participants.

Results Patients had significantly higher CSF TNF-α and other cytokine differences, but CSF cytokine levels were not associated with cortical TSPO PET levels in any grey matter region.

Conclusion Early schizophrenia shows generalised CSF cytokine dysregulation, unlikely to reflect microglial changes given the absence of association with TSPO PET.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your TSPO and neuroinflammation PET interests, showing a dissociation between peripheral-compartment cytokines and microglial imaging.
