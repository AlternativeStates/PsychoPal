Gender differences in depressive-like behavior in Parkinson's disease.

Mannino Deborah, Cucinotta Laura, Basilotta Rossella, Labellarte Davide, Esposito Emanuela, Campolo Michela, Paterniti Irene
Frontiers in pharmacology · 2026-10-21 · doi:10.3389/fphar.2026.1961464
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Parkinson'sPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling

Background Although sex differences have been described in PD progression, the mechanisms underlying gender-specific susceptibility to depressive symptoms remain poorly understood.

Methods This study investigated sex-dependent differences in depressive-like behaviors and their underlying molecular mechanisms in an in vivo model of 1-methyl-4-phenyl-1,2,3,6-tetrahydropyridine (MPTP)-induced dopaminergic degeneration, using adult male and female CD1 mice.

Results Our results demonstrated that MPTP administration induced nigrostriatal degeneration and α-synuclein accumulation in both sexes, with more pronounced alterations in males.

Conclusion Overall, our results highlight the importance of considering sex as a biological variable in PD research and support the development of personalized therapeutic strategies targeting non-motor symptoms.
