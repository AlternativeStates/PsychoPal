Lysergic Acid Diethylamide Induces Transcriptional Changes Opposing Signatures of Brain Ageing and Neurodegeneration.

Aurora Savino, Carla Liaci, Ilaria Bertani, Simona Rando, Mattia Camera, Giorgio R Merlo, Lidia Avalle, Valeria Poli, Nereo Kalebic, Francesco Iorio
Aging cell · 2026 · doi:10.1111/acel.70793
LSDDementia / Alzheimer'sPre-clinicalPeer reviewed#lsd#brain ageing#transcriptomics

Aim Asks whether LSD-induced transcriptional changes oppose gene expression signatures of brain ageing and dementia.

Methods Cross-species transcriptomic analysis of chronic LSD in rats and acute LSD in mice, compared with human prefrontal cortex ageing and dementia datasets, plus amyloid-beta experiments in primary cortical cultures.

Results LSD-induced profiles were strongly anti-correlated with human ageing signatures and, less strongly, with dementia signatures, and short-term LSD attenuated amyloid-beta-induced alterations.

Conclusion Provides a transcriptional rationale for investigating LSD and related compounds in brain ageing and neurodegeneration, pointing to mechanisms for future study.

Why it matters for you: Offers a transcriptional framework linking psychedelic neuroplasticity to ageing, complementing your plasticity-marker research.
