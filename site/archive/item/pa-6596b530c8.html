Triple network connectivity in opioid use disorder and ketamine treatment: an integrative review

Merel Verhoeven, Kynah Walston Jenkins, Peter Manza
NeuroImage Clinical · 2026-11-01 · doi:10.1016/j.nicl.2026.104092
Ketamine / esketaminefMRIDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Opioid use disorderHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyClinical trial / evidence synthesis#ketamine#opioid use disorder#resting-state fmri

Aim To test whether ketamine's neuroplastic effects act on salience, default mode and central executive network connectivity disrupted in opioid use disorder.

Methods Integrative review of resting-state fMRI studies on ketamine effects in MDD and on functional connectivity differences between OUD individuals and healthy adults.

Results Functional connectivity between the salience and default mode networks is reduced in OUD, while ketamine appears to increase it in MDD. In OUD this connectivity increases with abstinence.

Conclusion Salience-default mode connectivity is an intriguing marker of ketamine's mechanism that may support repurposing it for OUD.

Why it matters for you: Links ketamine's neuroplastic mechanisms to network connectivity, aligning with your work on ketamine and multimodal plasticity markers.
