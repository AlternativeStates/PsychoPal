Genome writing reveals SVA retrotransposon consequences in X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism.

Weimin Zhang, Yu Zhao, Priya Prakash, Heather Appleby, Kelly Barriball, Simona Capponi, Qingwen Jiang, Aleksandra M Wudzinska, Jordan R Westerdahl, Alan Mejia Maza, Kathryn Mangiameli, Christine A Vaine, Gwen Ellis, Neha Rahman, Stefan Markovic, Orin Mishkit, Kerry C Limberg, Matthew T Maurano, Youssef Z Wadghiri, Sang Y Kim, Vanessa C Wheeler, H T Marc Timmers, D Cristopher Bragg, Shane A Liddelow, Ran Brosh, Jef D Boeke
Cell · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.057
Parkinson'sPre-clinicalPeer reviewed#XDP#TAF1#retrotransposon

Aim How an intronic SVA retrotransposon in TAF1 contributes to X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism (XDP).

Methods Genomic rewriting of a portion of the mouse Taf1 allele with the 78-kb XDP patient-derived allele in mouse embryonic stem cells and mice, with chromatin and transcriptome profiling and CRISPRa activation.

Results The intronic SVA reduced hybrid TAF1 mRNA, downregulated TATA-box genes and triggered apoptosis. Intronic SVAs were actively transcribed and impeded elongation. Neuronal humanisation caused male lethality and striatal atrophy, and CRISPRa activation restored viability.

Conclusion Boosting TAF1 transcription may be a therapeutic approach for XDP, which is associated with striatal degeneration.

Why it matters for you: Striatal neurodegeneration and transcriptional mechanisms in a human disease model are loosely relevant to your neuroscience interests.
