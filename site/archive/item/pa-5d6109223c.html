Virtual reality as a neural state-modulating technology: EEG complexity during VR gameplay versus VR gameplay viewing

Nir Getter, Aviv Dotan, Amber Maimon, Oren Shriki
Computers in Human Behavior Reports · 2026-11-09 · doi:10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101446
Psychedelics (general)ClinicalPeer reviewedInformation theory / complexityBrain stimulation#eeg complexity#virtual reality#altered states

Aim Whether EEG signal complexity differs between actively playing immersive virtual reality and passively watching a recording of the same gameplay.

Methods Thirty-six adults randomised to play an immersive VR game or view a first-person gameplay video in the headset, with four EEG complexity measures and flow and ego dissolution questionnaires.

Results VR gameplay was associated with lower EEG complexity across four broadband measures, with large effects at frontal and parietal midline sites (all corrected p < .001). Flow was higher (d = 1.17) and exploratory ego dissolution higher (d = 1.11). Within-condition correlations did not survive correction.

Conclusion The authors conclude that VR can serve as an experimental route to studying how designed experiences shape conscious state, with neural and experiential effects assessed together.
