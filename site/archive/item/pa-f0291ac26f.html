DMT-induced aberrant salience and suggestibility correlate with mystical experiences and positive emotionality

Isabel Wießner, Marcelo Falchi-Carvalho, Sophie Laborde, Handersson Barros, Raynara Bolcont, Melanie Moura Medina Gurgel, Daniel C. Mograbi, Nicole Coelho, Fernanda Palhano-Fontes, Draulio de Araujo
Scientific Reports · 2026-11-23 · doi:10.1038/s41598-026-79990-1
LSDDMTPsychosis / schizophreniaHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling#dmt#aberrant salience#suggestibility

Aim To test whether DMT-induced aberrant salience and suggestibility relate to mystical and emotional psychedelic experiences.

Methods Randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind crossover study in 25 healthy participants receiving inhaled DMT (60 mg) or active placebo (0.6 mg), with aberrant salience, suggestibility and psychedelic experience questionnaires.

Results Compared with placebo, DMT increased all aberrant salience factors, most suggestibility modalities and most psychedelic factors. Aberrant salience correlated widely with psychedelic experiences, particularly mystical and positive emotional factors.

Conclusion DMT induces both psychosis- and therapy-relevant processes, with the link to mystical experience possibly indirect and emotionality playing a more central role.

Why it matters for you: Adds DMT evidence to the psychedelic clinical pharmacology strand, linking suggestibility and emotion to the mystical experiences your DMT work touches.
