Selective distal upper extremity recovery following targeted dentate nucleus temporal interference stimulation in stroke-double-blind, sham-controlled randomized controlled trial with resting-state EEG

Ailipinai Yasen, Zi-zhuo Xu, Jun Lu, Wanting Sun, Conghui Wei, Jinfa Feng, Yu‐Er Jiang, Ping-ting Liao, Yan Gong, Xiaoyu Liao
Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation · 2026-11-17 · doi:10.1186/s12984-026-02213-1
ClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformBrain stimulationClinical trial / evidence synthesis#temporal interference#stroke#resting-state eeg

Aim Whether dentate nucleus-targeted temporal interference stimulation improves upper limb recovery after subacute stroke, with resting-state EEG as the main neurophysiological outcome.

Methods Double-blind, sham-controlled randomised trial in 50 subacute stroke patients (47 completers), 10 sessions of active or sham tTIS combined with standard rehabilitation; primary outcomes FMA-UE and ARAT.

Results Active stimulation improved FMA-UE (adjusted mean difference 3.98, d = 0.85, p = 0.006) and ARAT (2.98, d = 1.01, p = 0.005), driven by distal control. Active participants showed increased global beta and ipsilateral frontal theta power; no safety problems occurred.

Conclusion DN-targeted tTIS was safe and feasible and produced short-term distal upper limb gains, with EEG changes that the authors report as exploratory.
