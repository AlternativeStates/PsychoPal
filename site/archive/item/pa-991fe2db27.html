Meditation-induced coherence and perennial aging.

Yamnath Poudel, Sailza Sailza, Paolo Grigolini
Frontiers in network physiology · 2026-11-12 · doi:10.3389/fnetp.2026.1948000
Peer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyInformation theory / complexityComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain–body physiology#brain-heart coupling#meditation#eeg complexity

Aim Examine how meditation alters scaling behaviour and complexity synchronisation between brain and heart dynamics, using a non-Markovian quantum-like framework.

Methods Theoretical and analytical study applying a non-ergodic self-organisation model to EEG and ECG during Naam Jap meditation; scaling exponent estimation, brain-heart complexity synchronisation and group-level statistics.

Results During meditation the scaling exponent δ decreased in both brain and heart dynamics, with greater brain-heart complexity synchronisation. After meditation δ rose above baseline, and an illustrative subject gave μ ≃ 1.83.

Conclusion Meditation induces a coherent state followed by increased physiological complexity, supporting a non-ergodic account of cognition, though the framework is novel and specific.
