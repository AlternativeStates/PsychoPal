Gender- and season-specific neural signature revealed by resting EEG in major depressive disorder

Jie Xiang, Yu Cui, Huazhi Li, Yan Niu, Mengni Zhou, Ji Dai
BMC Psychiatry · 2026-11-24 · doi:10.1186/s12888-026-08906-0
EEGDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformInformation theory / complexity#eeg complexity#major depressive disorder#biomarkers

Aim Determine how gender and season jointly modulate resting EEG activity in major depressive disorder and whether EEG features predict symptom severity.

Methods Resting-state EEG from 132 people with MDD (67 women, 65 men, aged 18.66-78.00 years) across seasons; power spectral density, fuzzy entropy and transfer entropy; two-way ANOVA and Categorical Boosting regression with SHAP analysis.

Results Neural activity was modulated by gender and season, with peak divergence in winter or summer for women and spring for men. EEG features predicted Beck Depression Inventory scores with high accuracy (r = 0.9405 for women in winter).

Conclusion EEG features may serve as candidate biomarkers, and diagnosis may need to account for seasonal and gender-specific biological rhythms.
