Radiosynthesis and evaluation of an imidazo[4,5-b]pyridin-2-one-derived radioligand for imaging GluN2B-containing NMDA receptors.

Yinlong Li, Zhendong Song, Taoqian Zhao, Haofeng Shi, Yanwei Jiang, Yuanfang Jiang, Jiahui Chen, Xin Zhou, Lingxin Meng, Ruihu Song, Zhenkun Sun, Siyan Feng, Hongjie Yuan, Achi Haider, Steven H Liang
American journal of nuclear medicine and molecular imaging · 2026-10-25 · doi:10.62347/aebg3929
PETDementia / Alzheimer'sParkinson'sPre-clinicalPeer reviewedPre-clinical / invasiveMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#pet radioligand#nmda receptor#glun2b

Aim To develop and evaluate an 18F-labelled PET radioligand for imaging GluN2B-containing NMDA receptors in the brain.

Methods Radiosynthesis of [18F]N2B-2502 by nucleophilic fluorination; in vitro autoradiography in rat brain sections; PET imaging in CD-1 mice with and without P-glycoprotein inhibition.

Results The tracer was obtained with >98% radiochemical purity and >37 GBq/μmol molar activity. Autoradiography showed uptake in hippocampus, thalamus, striatum and cortex. Brain uptake in mice was low and increased after P-gp inhibition.

Conclusion The compound appears to be a P-gp substrate with insufficient brain penetration, so further scaffold optimisation is needed before in vivo GluN2B imaging.

Why it matters for you: A radioligand for NMDA receptors is relevant to your PET work on ketamine and neuroplastogens, though it is at an early preclinical stage.
