Psychedelics and Sleep: Preclinical and Human Studies-A Systematic Review.

Kim Supit, Giordano N Rossi, José Augusto S Reis, Leman H Dursun, Serdar M Dursun, Jaime E C Hallak, Rafael G Dos Santos
Psychiatry and clinical psychopharmacology · 2026-10-11 · doi:10.65801/pcp.3606
PsilocybinDMTAyahuascaPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis

Background Classic psychedelics, including LSD, psilocybin, and N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT)/ayahuasca, have shown promise as rapid-acting treatments for psychiatric disorders.

Methods A systematic review was conducted following PRISMA guidelines. Study validity was assessed using appropriate methodological tools.

Results Preclinical studies consistently demonstrated acute REM sleep suppression, delayed REM onset, increased wakefulness, and transient disruption of non-REM sleep. Human findings were more heterogeneous, although randomized trials reported prolonged REM latency after psilocybin and reduced REM duration after ayahuasca.

Conclusion Classic psychedelics produce acute but transient alterations in sleep physiology, particularly affecting REM sleep.
