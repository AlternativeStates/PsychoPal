The spatial distribution of glioblastoma correlates with specific neurotransmitter maps

E Fabris, G Sansone, L Pini, M Corbetta, A Salvalaggio
Neuro-Oncology · 2026-11-23 · doi:10.1093/neuonc/noag245.517
Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modellingMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#neurotransmitter maps#pet#glioblastoma

Aim To test whether the spatial distribution of glioblastoma follows the normative neurotransmitter and receptor architecture of the healthy brain.

Methods Retrospective study of 96 glioblastoma patients (mean age 62.2 years) from Padova University Hospital, with parcel-wise lesion frequency maps compared to normative PET neurotransmitter maps across two atlases using Neuromaps and spatially constrained null models.

Results Glioblastoma frequency correlated with dopaminergic, serotonergic and cholinergic systems, including 5-HT4 (ρ = 0.57, qFDR = 0.028), D2/D3 (ρ = 0.62, qFDR = 0.029) and DAT (r = 0.47, qFDR = 0.003). Higher expression of these neurotransmitters was associated with more frequent tumour occurrence.

Conclusion The authors conclude that the brain's neurochemical architecture may contribute to the spatial distribution of gliomas, consistent with gliomas integrating into functional neural networks. The design is correlational.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your PET neurotransmitter mapping work, applying normative receptor maps to a clinical spatial question.
