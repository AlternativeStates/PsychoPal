A pectin-type acidic polysaccharides from Coix lachryma-jobi L. seeds alleviated depression-like behavior by modulating microbiota-gut-brain axis in CRS-induced rats.

Qianqian Yang, Lina Xu, Hanli Ye, Xingli Liu, Qinghai Zhang, Zhoubing Huang, Junxiao Pang, Dali Sun
Carbohydrate polymers · 2026-10-12 · doi:10.1016/j.carbpol.2026.125934
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling#gut-brain axis#polysaccharide#depression

Aim To determine whether a pectin-type polysaccharide from Coix seeds alleviates depression-like behaviour through the gut-brain axis in a chronic restraint stress rat model.

Methods Polysaccharide CSP-A isolated from Coix lachryma-jobi seeds and structurally characterised, then tested in chronic restraint stress rats with analysis of gut microbiota, short-chain fatty acids, colon pathology and metabolic profiles.

Results CSP-A reduced depressive-like behaviours, rebalanced gut microbiota, restored short-chain fatty acid levels and reduced colonic damage and inflammation. The authors propose an AhR/CYP1A1/BDNF pathway.

Conclusion The authors suggest CSP-A may act as a prebiotic with antidepressant potential, with the mechanism inferred from the structure and the animal data.

Why it matters for you: Only loosely relevant: it touches gut-brain signalling, which links to your interest in brain-body coupling, but is a preclinical nutritional study.
