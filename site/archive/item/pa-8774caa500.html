Psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression: fMRI-measured brain mechanisms

Robin Lester Carhart-Harris, Leor Roseman, Mark Bolstridge, Lysia Demetriou, Justine Nienke Pannekoek, Matthew B. Wall, Mark Tanner, Mendel Kaelen, John McGonigle, Kevin Murphy, Robert Leech, H. Valerie Curran, David John Nutt
Scientific Reports · 2017-10-13 · doi:10.1038/s41598-017-13282-7
PsilocybinfMRIDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling#psilocybin#depression#fmri

Aim To characterise the brain mechanisms, measured with fMRI, of psilocybin-assisted treatment for treatment-resistant depression.

Methods Pre and post-treatment fMRI measuring cerebral blood flow and BOLD resting-state connectivity in 19 patients treated with psilocybin and psychological support, with 16 having quality scans.

Results All 19 patients showed reduced depressive symptoms at one week, and 47% responded at five weeks. Reduced amygdala blood flow correlated with symptom reduction, and default-mode connectivity increased post-treatment.

Conclusion Post-treatment brain changes differ from acute psychedelic effects yet relate to clinical outcomes. The authors propose a reset mechanism, with small sample size as a limitation.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your psilocybin for depression work and to the default-mode and amygdala mechanisms you study.
