Efficacy Evaluation of Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation at Individual Frequency for Patients With Prolonged Disorders of Consciousness: A Preliminary Study Based on Mismatch Negative Amplitude.

Yu-Qin Zhao, Lin-Xi Li, Nuan Yang, Zi-Xin Li, Yu-Ting Zhang, Xiao-Guang Lang, Yue-Min Gao, Zhi-Xing Yao, Sui Tian, Ying Lv, Hong-Ling Li, Xue-Hai Lv
Brain and behavior · 2026 · doi:10.1002/brb3.71939
TMS / TMS-EEGDisorders of consciousnessClinicalPeer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive codingBrain stimulation#rtms#mismatch negativity#disorders of consciousness

Aim To test whether rTMS at an individually selected frequency, guided by mismatch negativity amplitude, improves consciousness in prolonged disorders of consciousness.

Methods Double-blind randomised controlled study of 29 patients; experimental group received individual frequency selection (5, 10 or 15 Hz) based on optimal MMN response, controls received fixed 10 Hz; 4 weeks of rTMS targeting the left mastoid alongside routine rehabilitation.

Results All groups improved. The 15 Hz group (n = 7) had the largest gains (CRS-R 4.71 to 9.29), with greater net improvement than controls (Z = -1.998, p = 0.046). MMN amplitude increased in the 5 Hz and 10 Hz experimental subgroups versus controls.

Conclusion The authors suggest individualised rTMS frequency guided by MMN may aid consciousness recovery and that MMN may be an objective biomarker. The very small subgroups and preliminary design limit inference.

Why it matters for you: Connects TMS and EEG-based biomarkers, a direct overlap with your TMS-EEG and neurodynamics methods.
