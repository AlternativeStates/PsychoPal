Magnetoencephalography reveals altered synaptic gain linked to functional impairment in adolescents at familial risk for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder

Oskar Hougaard Jefsen, Maja Gregersen, Sinnika Birkehøj Rohd, Ron Nudel, Daniel J. Hauke, Anette Faurskov Bundgaard, Andreas Færgemand Laursen, Lotte Veddum, Merete Birk, Kathrine Skak Madsen, William F.C. Baaré, Kit Melissa Larsen, Hartwig Roman Siebner, Torben E. Aim To test whether auditory oddball responses and effective connectivity differ in adolescents at familial high risk for schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.

Methods MEG during a roving oddball paradigm in 15-year-olds: 59 at familial high risk for schizophrenia, 38 for bipolar disorder, and 75 population controls; sensor and source analyses; dynamic causal modelling of effective connectivity.

Results Sensor and source-level responses did not differ significantly. DCM revealed reduced oddball-dependent pyramidal gain in a left frontal source in both high-risk groups, associated with lower functioning across groups.

Conclusion Biophysical modelling identifies surprise-related differences in effective connectivity, suggesting frontal synaptic gain alterations may be an early vulnerability marker.

Why it matters for you: Uses DCM and MEG to probe synaptic gain in early risk, matching your neurodynamics and computational psychiatry methods.
