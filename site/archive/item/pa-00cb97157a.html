Effects of heart rate variability biofeedback and relaxation training on ventromedial prefrontal cortex activity in young patients with depressive disorders: An EEG study.

Yi-Hui Lin, Andy Jeesu Kim, Mara Mather, Yi-Chun Yeh, Cheng-Fang Yen, Cian-Ruei Jian, Hui-Yuan Liao, Kuan-Lin Lee, Yi-Yu Chiang, Yi-Ting Lyu, Hsiao-Chun Liu, I-Mei Lin
Psychiatry research. Neuroimaging · 2026-11-01 · doi:10.1016/j.pscychresns.2026.112369
EEGDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedBrain–body physiology#hrv biofeedback#vmpfc#eeg

Aim Whether heart rate variability biofeedback changes ventromedial prefrontal cortex EEG activity more than relaxation training in young patients with depression.

Methods Randomised comparison of HRV biofeedback (n = 22 analysed) versus relaxation training (n = 23 analysed) over five weeks, twice weekly 60-minute sessions; eyes-closed EEG current source density in bilateral vmPFC across six frequency bands.

Results The biofeedback group showed greater right vmPFC beta3 and left vmPFC high-beta current source density at transfer than relaxation training. Both groups showed increased theta across stages.

Conclusion HRV biofeedback may engage vmPFC regulatory processes beyond the general relaxation effect, the authors suggest.

Why it matters for you: Pairs heart-based intervention with EEG source-level measures of prefrontal activity, which fits your interoception and EEG work.
