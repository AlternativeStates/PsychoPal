P045 | CLINICAL AND NEUROPHYSIOLOGICAL EFFECTS OF ANODAL TRANSCRANIAL DIRECT CURRENT STIMULATION IN PATIENTS WITH REFRACTORY CHRONIC CLUSTER HEADACHE

Systems Medicine
Confinia Cephalalgica · 2026-11-03 · doi:10.4081/cc.2026.16001
ClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyBrain stimulation#tdcs#cluster headache#eeg connectivity

Aim Assess clinical and resting-state EEG effects of anodal tDCS over left DLPFC in refractory chronic cluster headache.

Methods Fifteen patients received ten sessions of anodal tDCS (2 mA, 20 min) over C3 with cathode at FP2, with clinical scales and high-density resting EEG at baseline, post-treatment and one month later, analysed by network-based statistics.

Results MIDAS fell from 31.8 to 23.2 (p=0.01) and HIT-6 from 66.7 to 60.2 (p=0.01), with benefits persisting at one month. Connectivity showed reduced theta and increased alpha and low-gamma connectivity.

Conclusion The authors conclude that anodal tDCS was associated with sustained clinical improvement and cortical reorganisation, though these preliminary findings are uncontrolled.

Why it matters for you: Links to your interest in neuromodulation and EEG connectivity, though the design is preliminary and uncontrolled.
