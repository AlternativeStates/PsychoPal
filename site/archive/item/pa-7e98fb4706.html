Psilocybin-Assisted Therapy for Cancer-Related Psychological and Existential Distress in Adults with Cancer: An Integrative Systematic Review.

Athanasios Pitis, Maria Nikoloudi, Nikos Stefanis, Kyriaki Mystakidou
Journal of pain & palliative care pharmacotherapy · 2026-11-03 · doi:10.1080/15360288.2026.2756950
PsilocybinDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)AnxietyClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#psilocybin#cancer#existential distress

Aim Evaluates evidence on the safety, efficacy and therapeutic impact of psilocybin-assisted therapy for cancer-related psychological and existential distress.

Methods Integrative systematic review following PRISMA guidelines of 11 reports from six clinical cohorts, searched August to October 2025, with MMAT appraisal.

Results Single moderate-to-high psilocybin doses produced rapid, enduring reductions in depression, anxiety and existential distress lasting 2 to 6 months, and up to 4.5 years in some cases; no serious adverse events were reported.

Conclusion Psilocybin-assisted therapy may be promising for cancer-related distress but remains investigational, with several reports showing methodological limitations.

Why it matters for you: Synthesises clinical psilocybin evidence in a medical population, relevant to your psilocybin for depression and clinical pharmacology work.
