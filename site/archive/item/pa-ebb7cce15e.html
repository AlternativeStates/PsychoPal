A single-cell and spatial 3D multi-omic atlas of developing human basal ganglia.

Matthew G Heffel, Heng Xu, Oier Pastor-Alonso, Xinzhe Li, Mohammad S Baig, Rayyan Irfan Ghoor, Runjia Li, Colin Kern, Joonho Kum, Yi Zhang, Jon Paino, Gaurav Singh Rathore, Min Jen Tsai, Chu-Yi Tai, Grant Tucker, Zoey Zhao, Angie Hou, Zachary von Behren, Mohini Bhade, Siqian Li, Kadellyn Sandoval, Jessica Scholes, Felicia Codrea, Jeffrey Calimlim, Emma K Liao, Gwyneth Leung, JaeYeon Kim, Alexandra LeFevre, Robert Johnson, Thomas Blanchard, Eleazar Eskin, Jonathan Flint, Jennifer A Cotter, Bogdan Pasaniuc, Bogdan Bintu, Quan Zhu, Eran A Mukamel, Jason Ernst, Mercedes F Paredes, Chongyuan Luo
Cell · 2026 · doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.10.008
Peer reviewed

Background Human basal ganglia (BG) neurons and inhibitory interneurons originate in the ganglionic eminences (GEs), transient structures in the ventral prenatal brain.

Results This study delineates the specification and maturation of GEs and GE-derived neurons using single-nucleus methyl-3C sequencing (snm3C-seq), spatial transcriptomics, and single-molecule chromatin + RNA imaging, and we found heterogeneous temporal progression across GE subregions.

Conclusion Our study extends findings on unique 3D-epigenomic dynamics during human brain development.
