Utilizing Zebrafish to Better Understand Addiction and Psychedelic Treatment

Ethan V. Hagen
ERA: Education and Research Archive (University of Alberta) · 2026-11-17 · doi:10.7939/86161
Psychedelics (general)Nicotine / smokingAlcohol use disorderAnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling#zebrafish#lsd#addiction

Aim To establish a zebrafish model of addiction-like behaviour and to test whether LSD can modify ethanol- and nicotine-related behaviour.

Methods Adult and larval zebrafish; conditioned colour preference after ethanol exposure and withdrawal periods of 2, 4 or 8 days; open field and novel object tests after acute and repeated LSD doses; repeated ethanol and nicotine exposure with LSD applied on withdrawal day …

Results Summary not reported in the text provided; the abstract describes the behavioural paradigms, including conditioned colour preference, anxiety-like behaviour and boldness measures.

Conclusion The thesis aims to provide a more comprehensive zebrafish model of addiction-like behaviour and to assess LSD as a potential treatment. Findings are from a preclinical animal model.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your interest in psychedelic treatment of addiction, though the work is preclinical and does not use neuroimaging.
