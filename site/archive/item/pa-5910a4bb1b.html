Concurrent neuronal activity gates persistent dendritic spine shrinkage during mGluR-induced long-term depression

Eva Rojo Francàs, Noelia Sánchez-Rodríguez, Andrea Santuy, Katrín Hörpudóttir, J. Casado Flores, Antoni Parcerisas, Eduardo Soriano, Adam Ranson, Karen Zito, Miquel Bosch
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-23 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.21.765465
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPreprintConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modellingPre-clinical / invasive#mglur ltd#dendritic spines#synaptic plasticity

Aim To determine whether mGluR-dependent long-term depression is accompanied by persistent dendritic spine shrinkage, and what conditions govern this coupling.

Methods Organotypic rat hippocampal slice cultures; mGluR-LTD induced with the mGluR1/5 agonist DHPG; spine morphology and calcium transients monitored in CA1 pyramidal neurons; pharmacological manipulations and two-photon glutamate uncaging.

Results Persistent long-term spine shrinkage occurred with mGluR activation only under elevated neuronal activity, termed MIND-sLTD. It required mGluR1/5 and protein synthesis but not NMDA receptor signalling, and single-spine shrinkage was induced when mGluR activation was synchronised with glutamate uncaging.

Conclusion mGluR activation coincident with synaptic activity drives persistent structural depression, suggesting a coincidence-detection mechanism for long-term spine remodelling.

Why it matters for you: Links mGluR-LTD and spine structure to the plasticity and neuroplastogen mechanisms you study in humans, with a mechanistic rodent bridge.
