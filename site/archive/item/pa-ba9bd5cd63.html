Mapping Endogenous Psychedelic Chemistry: Indolethylamine N -Methyltransferase (INMT) Localizes Proximal to the Golgi Apparatus of Human Neurons

Karis Will, Sergio Lázaro Martínez, Surya A. Reis, Stephen J. Haggarty
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-02 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.30.758666
DMTPreprintConnectivity / synchrony#dmt#inmt#golgi

Aim To characterise INMT, the DMT-producing enzyme, at the protein level in human neurons and locate it within the cell.

Methods Validated anti-INMT antibody and immunocytochemistry in human iPSC-derived neurons, co-localising INMT, DMT-like and 5-HT2A receptor signals with the Golgi apparatus.

Results INMT localised close to other DMT biosynthetic enzymes at the cytosolic face of the cis-Golgi network across multiple Golgi phenotypes. DMT-like and 5-HT2A immunoreactivity partly localised near the Golgi.

Conclusion The cis-Golgi network may be a microdomain for psychedelic tryptamine biosynthesis, possibly creating a local pool of DMT accessing intracellular 5-HT2A receptors.

Why it matters for you: Speaks to your computational and membrane-permeation interests in tryptamines, with a plausible intracellular mechanism for endogenous DMT.
