A neural code for generative speech production in human Wernicke's area.

Alexander B Silva, Matthew K Leonard, Patrick W Hullett, Deborah F Levy, Catherine Wang, Duo Xu, Ilina Bhaya-Grossman, Edward F Chang
Science (New York, N.Y.) · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1126/science.aef8963
ClinicalPeer reviewedBrain stimulationMachine learning / AIPre-clinical / invasive#speech production#wernicke's area#intracranial recording

Aim Does Wernicke's area, classically linked to speech comprehension, also contribute to generating speech, and if so, what does it encode?

Methods Direct intracranial recordings in human patients, cortical stimulation and lesion analysis, examining activity before speech and causal necessity for production.

Results A discrete subzone, the left posterior superior temporal gyrus, activates before speech, is causally necessary for production, and encodes features of whole upcoming words rather than low-level acoustics or articulation. Its activity differs from nearby auditory-feedback responses and can decode intended speech in people with paralysis.

Conclusion Wernicke's area contains a dedicated subregion for speech generation alongside its role in auditory processing.

Why it matters for you: Adds a causal human-cortex perspective on how a region's perceptual role coexists with production, relevant to your interest in neurodynamics and whole-brain organisation.
