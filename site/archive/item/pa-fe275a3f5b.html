Tracking single neurons across days reveals acute and persistent effects of psilocybin in the mouse brain

Tate E DeWeese, Alexandra Cheng, Sai Koukuntla, Kellie L. Tamashiro, T.D. Harris, Austin R. Graves
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.14.766632
PsilocybinDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPreprintPre-clinical / invasive#psilocybin#single neurons#neuroplasticity

Aim Whether psilocybin's effects on single-neuron firing extend beyond the acute drug presence, and how acute responses relate to lasting changes.

Methods Chronically implanted Neuropixels probes in mice tracked 1,692 neurons in prefrontal and hippocampal regions after saline and psilocybin injections and again 24 h later.

Results Psilocybin bidirectionally modulated firing acutely in a cell-type and region-specific manner. Firing 24 h post-injection was net suppressed relative to saline, including in acutely activated neurons, and the late acute response predicted later firing.

Conclusion The authors present the first direct evidence of psilocybin's sustained effects on neuronal firing, offering insight into circuitry behind its lasting alterations.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your psilocybin work and to the link between acute drug effects and lasting neuroplasticity you study with PET and EEG.
