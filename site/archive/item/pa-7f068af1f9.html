The 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist/antagonist volinanserin modulates motor outcomes and regulates lateral septum-prefrontal and lateral septum-hippocampal functional connectivity.

Amelia Bryers, Cheryl Hawkes, Edward Parkin, Neil Dawson
Journal of psychopharmacology (Oxford, England) · 2026-11-10 · doi:10.1177/02698811261495549
fMRIAnxietyPsychosis / schizophreniaPre-clinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchrony#5-ht2a#volinanserin#functional connectivity

Aim How the 5-HT2A inverse agonist volinanserin affects behaviour and circuit-level brain activity and connectivity in mice, relevant to schizophrenia.

Methods Behavioural battery in mice covering motor, anxiety-like, compulsive-like, reward and learning domains, with brain activity and functional connectivity measured for drug versus vehicle.

Results Volinanserin selectively reduced locomotor activity, leaving anxiety, compulsive-like behaviour, reward and learning unaffected. It raised activity in the lateral septum, dorsal raphe and nucleus accumbens, lowered prefrontal activity, and reduced lateral septum connectivity with prefrontal cortex, hippocampus and amygdala.

Conclusion The authors conclude the lateral septum is a key locus for volinanserin's putative antipsychotic effects, supporting its role in 5-HT2A-targeting drug action.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to 5-HT2A pharmacology and circuit-level connectivity, though it is a mouse study outside your human imaging work.
