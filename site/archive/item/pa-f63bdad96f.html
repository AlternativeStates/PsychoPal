Immediate transcranial magnetic stimulation-evoked potentials reveal short-latency intracortical inhibition.

Lasse Christiansen, Yufei Song, Ditte Haagerup, Mikkel Malling Beck, Kora T Montemagno, John Rothwell, Hartwig Roman Siebner
The Journal of physiology · 2026-11-18 · doi:10.1113/jp291582
TMS / TMS-EEGHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive codingBrain stimulation#tms-eeg#sici#intracortical inhibition

Aim Test whether immediate TMS-evoked EEG potentials give a more direct measure of short-latency intracortical inhibition than MEP-based SICI.

Methods Sixteen healthy volunteers received the standard SICI paired-pulse protocol over motor cortex while immediate TEPs (1–10 ms) and MEPs were recorded, with SICF interval conditions compared.

Results SICI suppressed later iTEP components but not the earliest, and suppression was similar at SICF peaks and troughs. iTEP suppression correlated with MEP suppression, while N15 suppression behaved differently.

Conclusion The authors conclude that MEP-based SICI does not index global motor cortical inhibition but reflects circuit-specific inhibition that iTEPs can probe more directly.

Why it matters for you: Bears directly on your TMS-EEG methods and interest in measuring cortical inhibition noninvasively.
