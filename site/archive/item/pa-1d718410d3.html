Physiological Mechanisms of Yoga on Sleep Architecture and Autonomic Regulation: A Systematic Review of Randomized Controlled Trials

Yogesh Muthuraj, Sukumaran Clementswami, Jesudin Rajesh E, Saravanan Sivasamy
Aathiyoga Indian Journal of Ancient Medicine and Yoga · 2026-11-10
Opioid use disorderClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformBrain–body physiologyClinical trial / evidence synthesis#yoga#heart rate variability#sleep

Aim Systematically review randomised trials of yoga's physiological effects on sleep architecture and autonomic regulation, using objective endpoints.

Methods PRISMA 2020 systematic review of PubMed records from the past five years; four RCTs met criteria; extracted heart rate variability, cardiorespiratory, neuroendocrine, EEG and sleep onset latency data; Cochrane RoB 2 risk-of-bias assessment.

Results Multi-session yoga with pranayama increased high-frequency HRV, lowered the LF/HF ratio and reduced sleep onset latency by 61.08 minutes in adults in opioid withdrawal. Single sessions lowered respiratory rate but did not change EEG alpha power, acute sleep latency or salivary cortisol.

Conclusion Multi-session yoga shows autonomic and sleep benefits, whereas single acute sessions produced limited immediate physiological change; the evidence base is small.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your brain-body coupling and interoception interests, though the trials are small and mostly outside imaging.
