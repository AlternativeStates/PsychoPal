Semaglutide for the Treatment of Obesity in Children 6 to <12 Years of Age.

Ania M Jastreboff, Nina M Harder-Lauridsen, Michelle Van Name, Antje Körner, Aaron S Kelly, Daniel Weghuber, Jens-Christian Holm, Christine Frithioff-Bøjsøe, Shilpa Middolu, Tina Buch, Martin Wabitsch, STEP Young Investigators
The New England journal of medicine · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.1056/nejmoa2604058
ClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis

Background Once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide is approved for the treatment of obesity in adolescents and adults, but data for its use in young children are lacking.

Methods We conducted a phase 3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving children (6 to <12 years of age) with obesity.

Results At week 68, the mean percent change in BMI was -13.5% with semaglutide and 2.7% with placebo (estimated between-group difference, -16.2 percentage points; 95% confidence interval [CI], -19.4 to -12.9; P<0.001).

Conclusion Among children (6 to <12 years of age) with obesity, treatment with semaglutide for 68 weeks resulted in a significantly greater mean percent reduction in BMI than placebo.
