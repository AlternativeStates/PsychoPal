Changes in wellbeing, self-compassion and alexithymia in a proof of principle trial with MDMA-assisted therapy for major depressive disorder.

Mark Berthold-Losleben, Ivar W Goksøyr, Tor-Morten Kvam, Justyna Rog, Lowan H Stewart, Morgan Scarth, Ole A Andreassen
Journal of psychiatric research · 2026-11-07 · doi:10.1016/j.jpsychires.2026.11.014
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Substance use (other)PTSDClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingClinical trial / evidence synthesis#mdma-assisted therapy#depression#alexithymia

Aim To examine whether MDMA-assisted therapy changes alexithymia, self-compassion and well-being in major depressive disorder, and whether effects last.

Methods Open-label proof-of-principle trial with 12 participants with moderate to severe MDD (baseline MADRS 29.6), receiving two MDMA sessions with manualised psychotherapy; outcomes at baseline, 8 weeks and at least 3 months, analysed with linear mixed models.

Results TAS-20 fell by 11.75 post-treatment and 16.42 at follow-up (both p < 0.001). Self-compassion rose (SCS +0.73, p = 0.017; +1.07 at follow-up, p = 0.001). WEMWBS improved by 17.30 at both timepoints (p < 0.001).

Conclusion The authors conclude MDMA-assisted therapy produced lasting gains in alexithymia, self-compassion and well-being beyond symptom reduction. The trial is open-label, small and without a control group.

Why it matters for you: Adds a transdiagnostic angle to psychedelic-assisted treatment of depression, relevant to your work on psychedelics for depression.
