Reduced mismatch negativity to jittered frequency deviants but preserved gamma-band power in first episode psychosis.

E G Rowe, M Haaf, J Adams, K Allott, T Bridson, M Cai, M De Rozario, I Dzafic, M I Garrido, M Jacob, S Lavoie, C Pantelis, J Reeves, A Stainton, A Thompson, G Leicht, S J Wood
Psychiatry research · 2026-11-24 · doi:10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117605
Psychosis / schizophreniaHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformERP / MMN / predictive coding#mismatch negativity#gamma#psychosis

Aim Whether resting-state gamma power and mismatch negativity differ in first episode psychosis and relate to symptom severity.

Methods 64-channel EEG in 31 young people with first episode psychosis and 27 healthy controls aged 15-25, measuring resting gamma power and mismatch negativity to pitch and duration deviants using a jittered interstimulus interval paradigm.

Results No group differences in gamma power. Duration MMN did not differ, but frequency MMN was attenuated in the psychosis group. No correlations with symptom severity or functioning.

Conclusion Responses to frequency deviants are reduced in first episode psychosis without accompanying gamma changes, and neither measure indexes symptom severity or functional decline.

Why it matters for you: Uses E/I-related EEG markers in a clinical population, relevant to your interest in computational psychiatry and neurodynamics.
