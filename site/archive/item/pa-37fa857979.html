A novel theta-frequency tACS approach for major depressive disorder: Initial findings from an open-label pilot study.

Efstathia S Gotsis, Jeydhurga Raveendran, Neil W Bailey, Shona K Brinley, Bernadette M Fitzgibbon, Paul B Fitzgerald
Journal of psychiatric research · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.1016/j.jpsychires.2026.11.009
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformBrain stimulationClinical trial / evidence synthesis#tacs#theta oscillations#depression

Aim Whether individualised theta-frequency tACS over the prefrontal cortex is feasible and tolerable in moderate-to-severe major depressive disorder, and what clinical and EEG changes accompany it.

Methods Open-label pilot of 11 adults with MDD receiving 20 sessions of bilateral prefrontal theta tACS (1.5 mA peak-to-peak, 20 min) during working memory tasks, with frequency individualised from task EEG; 10 completed treatment.

Results MADRS scores fell significantly from baseline to midpoint, endpoint and follow-up. Response rates were 80% and remission 60% at endpoint, rising to 80% and 70% at follow-up. Greater ITF power increases tracked larger MADRS reductions, though group-level power changes were not significant.

Conclusion The authors conclude that the approach is feasible and provides preliminary observations to inform sham-controlled trials, cautioning that the open-label design and small sample limit interpretation and allow placebo effects.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your interest in theta oscillations and neurodynamic markers, and to TMS-EEG and EEG-based biomarkers in depression.
