Stress-induced dorsal striatal dopamine release is associated with changes in functional connectivity to the prefrontal cortex in nicotine-dependent individuals-a preliminary report.

Siyan Fan, Su Jin Kim, Yasmin Zakiniaeiz, Jocelyn Hoye, Bart de Laat, Alaaddin Ibrahimy, Gelsina Stanley, Chiang-Shan R Li, Kelly P Cosgrove, Michelle Hampson, Evan D Morris
Frontiers in neuroscience · 2026-10-27 · doi:10.3389/fnins.2026.1946892
fMRINicotine / smokingClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)

Background The neural mechanism linking stress to addiction pathophysiology remains unclear.

Methods Using a within-subject, multimodal design, we collected PET and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) scans in 8 smokers (6 females; mean age: 32 ± 3.6 years).

Results Cortisol increases correlated with dorsal striatal DA release (r = 0.708, p = 0.04). FC analyses revealed enhanced FC between dorsal striatum and ventral medial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) (p < 0.001, FDR-corrected), and between whole striatum and dorsal lateral PFC (dlPFC) (p < 0.001, FDR-corrected).
