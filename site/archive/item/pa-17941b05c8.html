Psychedelic experience intensity during ketamine treatment and antidepressant outcomes.

David S Mathai, Sean P Goldy, Thomas D Hull, Kristin Arden, Jack Swain, Leonardo Vando, Matteo Malgaroli
Psychopharmacology · 2026-11-18 · doi:10.1007/s00213-026-07219-0
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewed#ketamine#depression#subjective effects

Aim Test whether subjective psychedelic intensity during ketamine treatment is associated with antidepressant outcomes beyond an adverse-event framing.

Methods Retrospective longitudinal cohort of 3,637 adults with moderate-to-severe depression receiving up to four sublingual ketamine doses over four weeks, with subjective effects measured by the five-item Mindbloom Psychedelic Intensity scale at midpoint.

Results Higher intensity scores at two weeks were associated with greater PHQ-9 reduction at four weeks, higher likelihood of clinically significant change, and greater membership of an improvement trajectory, independent of dose and covariates.

Conclusion Subjective intensity of the ketamine experience is robustly associated with antidepressant response, although the retrospective design limits causal inference.

Why it matters for you: Links subjective ketamine effects to treatment response, relevant to your work on ketamine and psychedelics as neuroplastogens in depression.
