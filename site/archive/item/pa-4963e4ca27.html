Neurophysiological correlates of interoceptive compassion training for depression

Alessandra Anzolin, Seneca Ellis, Eunmi Kim, Diane Joss, Frannie Marin, Richa Gawande, Alexandra Comeau, Clare Bumpus, Josh Phillips, B. Rael Cahn, Misan W.D. Kim, Vitaly Napadow, Zev Schuman‐Olivier
Scientific Reports · 2026-11-15 · doi:10.1038/s41598-026-78490-6
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformERP / MMN / predictive codingBrain–body physiologyClinical trial / evidence synthesis#interoception#heartbeat-evoked potentials#depression

Aim Whether a four-week interoceptive compassion programme, HST-IIP, changes depression, heartbeat-evoked potentials and Delta activity in people with depression.

Methods Pilot randomised controlled trial of 23 adults with depression, HST-IIP versus waitlist control, with EEG and clinical assessments before and after a four-week intervention.

Results HST-IIP produced greater depression score improvement than control (Hedges' g = -1.422, p = 0.028) and increased interoceptive awareness. Attenuated right frontal heartbeat-evoked potentials and reduced Delta power during compassion meditation were associated with clinical improvement.

Conclusion The authors describe the work as exploratory preliminary evidence; the programme may influence neurophysiological processes relevant to depression, warranting further research.
