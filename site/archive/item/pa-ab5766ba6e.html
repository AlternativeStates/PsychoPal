Comprehensive benchmarking of EEG biomarkers for Parkinson's disease: robustness, reliability, and clinical discriminability across multiple datasets.

Sepideh Hajipour Sardouie, Mahsa Naseri, Mahdi Babaei, Zahra Kavian, Shervin Mehrtash, Mohamad Hosein Faramarzi, Maryam S Mirian, Martin James McKeown
Journal of neural engineering · 2026-11-05 · doi:10.1088/1741-2552/aebe69
EEGParkinson'sClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformInformation theory / complexityPreprocessing / source / toolsMachine learning / AI

Background Electroencephalography (EEG) provides a high-temporal-resolution, noninvasive window into some of the abnormalities underlying Parkinson's disease (PD), yet reported electrophysiological biomarkers vary widely across studies due to differences in preprocessing, feature extraction …

Methods Here we provide the first comprehensive, multi-dataset benchmarking of 16 EEG-derived biomarkers, including phase-amplitude coupling, beta burst metrics, aperiodic components, complexity measures, and beta waveform features, to evaluate their robustness, reliability, and clinical discriminability.

Results In contrast, oscillatory biomarkers, including beta-gamma phase-amplitude coupling, burst-derived metrics, and waveform-asymmetry features, showed substantial variability across methodological choices and lower within-session consistency. Fully-denoised data improved group-level separability, for several biomarkers in Dataset 1.

Conclusion Together, these findings identify several biomarkers with comparatively strong methodological robustness for further biological and clinical validation and highlight methodological factors that contribute to inconsistencies in the PD literature.
