Autistic adults’ attitudes toward ketamine, MDMA, LSD, psilocybin and antidepressants: a cross-sectional survey

Siddharth Vashi, Jonathon Marinis, Adam John Guastella, Nick Glozier
BMC Psychiatry · 2026-11-27 · doi:10.1186/s12888-026-08941-x
PsilocybinKetamine / esketamineClinicalPeer reviewed#psilocybin#autism#attitudes

Aim Describe how Autistic adults perceive harm, efficacy and willingness to try psilocybin, MDMA, ketamine, LSD and antidepressants.

Methods Cross-sectional anonymous online survey of 128 Autistic adults (October 2021 to July 2022), rating each substance, with paired comparisons and exploratory associations with self-medication.

Results Psilocybin had the highest proportions endorsing no harm, high efficacy and willingness to try, and differed from the other substances on several measures. Willingness was 17.2 to 21.1 points higher under doctor than peer recommendation.

Conclusion Autistic adults reported more favourable attitudes to psilocybin than antidepressants; these are perceptions, not evidence of comparative efficacy, and recommendation source may matter.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to the clinical translation of psychedelic therapies you work towards, in a population rarely studied in this area.
