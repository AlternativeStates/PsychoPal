Proteomic profiling of postmortem prefrontal cortex identifies molecular signatures of borderline personality disorder

E. Zillich, D. Avetyan, C. C. Witt, L. Zillich, J. Frank, F. Stein, J. J. Schwarz, N. Mechawar, G. Turecki, R. Spanagel, F. Streit, S. H. Witt
medrxiv (psychiatry and clinical psychology) · 2026-11-10 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.09.26367584
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)PTSDPreprintConnectivity / synchrony#borderline personality disorder#proteomics#prefrontal cortex

Aim To characterise proteomic signatures of borderline personality disorder in postmortem prefrontal cortex.

Methods Label-free proteomics of postmortem prefrontal cortex in 79 individuals (36 with BPD, 43 controls); differential expression, co-expression network, druggability and drug-repositioning analyses; integration with PTSD and MDD datasets.

Results Dysregulation of mitochondrial, immunological and neuroplasticity pathways was observed. A BPD-associated module enriched for secretory and synaptic processes was druggable by SSRIs, and STX7 and RTN4 were conserved differentially expressed proteins across BPD, PTSD and MDD.

Conclusion The study provides initial proteomic insights into BPD in the brain, suggests VEGF signalling as a possible target, and shows convergent signatures with common comorbidities.

Why it matters for you: Offers brain proteomic and synaptic signatures in a disorder adjacent to your computational psychiatry and mental health interests.
