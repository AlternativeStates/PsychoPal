Scn2a -deficient mice exhibit serotonergic dysfunction and altered behavioral responses to psilocybin.

Brody A Deming, Jingliang Zhang, Josephine Brown-Leung, Manasi Halurkar, Akila D Abeyaratna, Rineet Ranga, Keeley Farmer, Deepti Ramasamy, Jason Cannon, Yang Yang
bioRxiv : the preprint server for biology · 2026-11-04 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.03.764054
PsilocybinAnxietyPre-clinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveform#psilocybin#scn2a#autism

Aim Tests whether Scn2a deficiency alters behavioural responses to psilocybin and serotonergic system function in mice.

Methods Scn2a-deficient and wild-type mice given psilocybin, assessed for sociability, anxiety-like behaviour and head twitch response, with mPFC serotonin levels and receptor expression, and dorsal raphe nucleus firing measured.

Results Psilocybin increased sociability only in Scn2a-deficient mice and reduced head twitches; baseline mPFC serotonin did not differ, but receptor expression and dorsal raphe spontaneous firing were altered.

Conclusion Scn2a deficiency is associated with serotonergic alterations and differential psilocybin responses, suggesting serotonergic interventions may ameliorate social deficits in SCN2A-related autism.

Why it matters for you: Shows genotype-dependent psilocybin responses via serotonergic circuitry, relevant to your interest in serotonergic psychedelic pharmacology.
