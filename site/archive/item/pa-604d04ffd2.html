Rotigotine Behenate ameliorates depression in Parkinson's disease through NF-κB-mediated anti-inflammation and BDNF-mediated neuroprotection.

Mengting Wu, Zhicong Yin, Yumeng Chen, Caiyi Qin, Mengting Zheng, Ronghao Zhang, Wenjing Lv, Yuanyingzhu Liang, Jingwei Tian, Xin Yu
International immunopharmacology · 2026-11-15 · doi:10.1016/j.intimp.2026.117696
SSRIs / antidepressantsDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Parkinson'sPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingPre-clinical / invasive

Background Depression in Parkinson's disease (DPD) is a prevalent non-motor complication that currently lacks effective targeted therapy.

Methods We established a combined MPTP/CUMS model in male C57BL/6 mice, which induced a higher incidence of depression and more stable depressive-like phenotypes than MPTP-only modeling and closely recapitulated clinical DPD pathology.

Results Behavioral assays demonstrated that ROTB ameliorated both motor dysfunction and depressive-like behavior and restored regional neurotransmitter balance, whereas fluoxetine-included solely as an efficacy comparator for the depressive-like phenotype-did not improve motor symptoms. Mechanistically, ROTB suppressed NF-κB/NLRP3-mediated neuroinflammation and activated the BDNF/TrkB/PI3K/Akt/GSK-3β cascade, elevating synaptic protein levels and reversing dendritic spine loss.

Conclusion In summary, ROTB alleviates DPD through NF-κB-mediated anti-inflammation and BDNF-mediated neuroprotection, offering preclinical evidence for DPD therapy.
