Domain-level symptom improvement after ketamine and TMS-based stimulation in treatment-resistant depression: a post hoc pooled analysis.

Kuan-I Liu, Tung-Ping Su, Chih-Ming Cheng, Wei-Chen Lin, Ya-Mei Bai, Shih-Jen Tsai, Cheng-Ta Li, Mu-Hong Chen
Psychiatry research · 2026-11-07 · doi:10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117571
Ketamine / esketamineTMS / TMS-EEGDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)AnxietyClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain stimulationClinical trial / evidence synthesis#ketamine#tms#treatment-resistant depression

Aim Whether ketamine and TMS produce different short-term improvements across depression, anxiety, insomnia and somatic symptom domains.

Methods Post hoc pooled analysis of 159 active-treatment participants from three trials, ketamine infusion (n=89) or two-week TMS (n=70), with domain-level models adjusted for baseline scores, age and sex.

Results Improvement differed across domains within both modalities. Depression improved most (1.48 SD after ketamine, 1.71 after TMS). Between-modality contrasts did not survive adjustment, except the somatic contrast after adjusting for treatment-resistance categories.

Conclusion The authors caution that cross-modality comparisons are exploratory and do not establish equivalence or guide treatment choice.
