An engineered nanobody inhibitor for molecular-to-circuit control of opioid receptors

Zoé Valbret, Paul J. Lamothe‐Molina, Sofia Papadogkonaki, Antoine Koehl, Vincent Pascoli, Jan Vincent V. Arafiles, Ziva Shapiro Tuchman, Nicole M. Fisher, Eva Xia Wang, Arthur Radoux-Mergault, Annika Canziani, Gao-Yang Huang, Mark von Zastrow, Mickey Kosloff, Christian Lüscher, Christian P. R. Hackenberger, Aashish Manglik, Tommaso Patriarchi, Miriam Stoeber
Nature Chemical Biology · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1038/s41589-026-02390-z
Opioid use disorderPre-clinicalPeer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive coding#opioid receptors#nanobody#vta dopamine

Aim Whether an engineered intracellular nanobody can selectively inhibit active opioid receptors and control their signalling in defined cells.

Methods Structure-guided evolution of intracellular nanobodies, functional optimisation, and testing in cells, VTA neurons and mice, including cell-type-specific expression in VTA GABAergic neurons with fentanyl challenge.

Results Nb64 rapidly suppressed transducer engagement, receptor internalisation and μOR-driven GIRK currents. Cell-type-specific expression attenuated fentanyl-evoked dopamine release and behavioural responses.

Conclusion Nb64 offers a general strategy for precise in vivo inhibition of native GPCR signalling, though its use across other settings remains to be established.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your interest in opioid and reward circuitry and in tools for dissecting receptor signalling in VTA and dopamine systems.
