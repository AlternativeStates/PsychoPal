Fine and ultrafine Martian dust presents additional risks for future crewed missions to Mars

Scott J. V. VanBommel, Abigail L. Knight, Andrea D. Harrington, Sydney Do, C. D. K. Herd, Yang Liu, Jeff A. Berger, B. C. Clark, Steph A. Connell, W. T. Elam, Elisabeth M. Hausrath, M. H. Hecht, J. Background This knowledge gap is critical as these respirable particles, defined by their ability to bypass upper respiratory defenses, possess the mobility to penetrate deep into the alveolar regions of biological systems, presenting …

Methods Here, we report on direct examination of these fine and ultrafine Martian dust fractions using X-ray fluorescence through the analysis and modeling of data acquired by the Perseverance rover.

Results We observe particulates measuring as small as 100 to 200 nm on spacesuit samples and calibration targets. Compositionally, this dust is a phosphorus-, iron-, and sulfur-enriched mafic silicate accumulating on materials analogous to the planned external layers of potential crew systems.

Conclusion Our results emphasize the importance of a thorough characterization of Martian dust properties in terrestrial laboratories to better prepare future crews and crew support systems destined for the surface of Mars.
