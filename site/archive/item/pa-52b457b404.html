A reproducible pipeline for simulating individual and inter-brain neural dynamics from structural connectivity.

Annemarie Wolff, Mahta Ramezanian Panahi, Imme Christina Schultheiß, Juha M Lahnakoski, Kai Vogeley, Marc Tittgemeyer, Leonhard Schilbach, Guillaume Dumas
Journal of neuroscience methods · 2026-11-05 · doi:10.1016/j.jneumeth.2026.110943
MRI (structural/diffusion)ClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyInformation theory / complexityComputational / whole-brain modellingMachine learning / AIMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)

Background Computational models can link anatomical structure to neural dynamics.

Results In a test sample of individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and matched controls (16 each), simulated intra-individual synchrony added classification information beyond anatomy, raising accuracy from 71.2% (AUC 0.843) to 77.6% (AUC 0.871), in a sample where groups did not differ in mean structural connectivity.

Conclusion The pipeline provides a reproducible, extensible framework for deriving informative individual-level dynamic features from anatomy.
