A Translational Porcine Model Linking Brain 5-HT2A Receptor Occupancy to Psychedelic-like Behaviour

Clara Aabye Madsen, Miriam L. Navarro, Elisabeth Kolesnik, Julie F. M. Wedel-Heinen, Caroline Degel, Vanessa Drejfeldt, Virginia Camporesi, Despoina Kazani, Vladimir Shalgunov, Merlin Zabrocki, Matthias M. Herth, Dino Luethi, Friederike Holze, Matthias E. Liechti, Christoffer Bundgaard, Johan Juhl Weisser, Sys Stybe Johansen, Claus Svarer, Brice Ozenne, Hanne Demant Hansen, Gitte M. Knudsen
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-25 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.16.767031
Non-hallucinogenic analoguesPETPre-clinicalPreprintComputational / whole-brain modellingPre-clinical / invasiveMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#5-ht2a#pet#translational model

Aim Establish a translational pig model that links drug dose and plasma concentration to brain 5-HT2A receptor occupancy, so that behavioural doses can be chosen pharmacologically.

Methods Pigs were dosed with five 5-HT2A agonists and two antagonists; brain 5-HT2A occupancy was measured with [11C]Cimbi-36 PET and in vitro, and headshake behaviour was recorded at occupancy levels tied to psychedelic experience in humans.

Results Occupancy correspondence with human data was good across compounds. At human-relevant occupancy, all five agonists induced headshakes, including lisuride and 2-Br-LSD, which are otherwise considered non-psychedelic.

Conclusion Human-relevant 5-HT2A occupancy produces psychedelic-like behaviour in pigs, validating the model for translational dosing of novel 5-HT2A compounds.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your [11C]Cimbi-36 PET work and the question of which occupancy levels are psychedelic in humans.
