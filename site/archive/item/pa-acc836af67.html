Multi-receptor whole-brain modeling reveals affinity-driven chemoarchitectural mechanism of virtual atomoxetine action.

Martí Monge-Asensio, Murat Demirtaş, Thomas Pfeffer, Gorka Zamora-López, Jakub Vohryzek, Yonatan Sanz-Perl, Ruud Lucas van den Brink, Tobias H Donner, Morten L Kringelbach, Gustavo Deco
Frontiers in computational neuroscience · 2026-11-04 · doi:10.3389/fncom.2026.1854866
fMRIPre-clinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling#whole-brain modelling#atomoxetine#receptor maps

Aim To explain how atomoxetine reshapes whole-brain dynamics by linking multi-receptor adrenergic and dopaminergic organisation to resting-state functional connectivity.

Methods Pharmacological resting-state fMRI under atomoxetine and placebo combined with a whole-brain model incorporating cortical adrenergic and dopaminergic receptor expression, using receptor similarity and forward simulation.

Results Atomoxetine stabilised connectivity dynamics within a posterior catecholaminergic network. Model parameters converged toward empirical receptor binding affinities, and the drug state was reconstructed from placebo by negative reweighting along that affinity axis.

Conclusion Receptor binding affinity may be a key organising principle linking molecular receptor organisation to brain dynamics under catecholaminergic perturbation.

Why it matters for you: Matches your whole-brain modelling and computational psychiatry interests, applied to a pharmacological fMRI dataset.
