Scale-rich network-based metamaterials.

Csaba Both, Andrew Yen-Jong Chen, Ting-Ting Gao, Niek Mooij, Mohammad Charara, Jayna Wadhwa, Carlos M Portela, Albert-László Barabási
Nature · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.1038/s41586-026-11191-2
Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling#metamaterials#network science#multiscale

Aim To embed network heterogeneity into mechanical metamaterials and test whether multiscale variability yields properties unavailable to single-scale designs.

Methods Two-parameter scale-rich network models translated into physical materials, evaluated through simulations and experiments on elastic, nonlinear, dynamic and wave-control behaviour.

Results Scale-rich metamaterials showed highly tunable elastic anisotropy, delocalised nonlinear deformation with high energy absorption, enhanced energy dissipation under dynamic loading and programmable elastic-wave control.

Conclusion The framework provides a minimal, universal model for engineering multifunctional materials whose mechanics emerge from scale diversity, and opens new questions for network science.

Why it matters for you: Loosely relevant: the idea that function arises from multiscale connectivity echoes your interest in brain networks and neurodynamics.
