Characterizing source-level electroencephalography activities to single-pulse TMS-EEG.

Shengqiao Wang, Xiaoxiao Huang, Yujun Wang, Zhimin Huang, Jiumei Li, Xianwei Che
Clinical neurophysiology : official journal of the International Federation of Clinical Neurophysiology · 2026-11-13 · doi:10.1016/j.clinph.2026.2112473
EEGTMS / TMS-EEGHealthy volunteersPre-clinicalPeer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive codingBrain stimulationPreprocessing / source / tools#tms-eeg#source localisation#dlpfc

Aim Whether source-level EEG measures distinguish active from sham single-pulse TMS over the left DLPFC at early and late latencies.

Methods Thirty-three healthy adults underwent active and sham TMS-EEG; analyses included TEPs, electric-field simulations, source-level SCD and SCS in target ROIs, whole-brain cluster permutation tests, and peripheral covariate adjustment after ICA.

Results Active stimulation gave larger early local TEPs (2.15 vs 0.85 μV) and greater target-region SCS and SCD in both windows. Significant whole-brain clusters appeared only at late latencies (about 270–375 ms), and one SCD cluster did not survive muscle-related adjustment.

Conclusion Target-region source measures separated active from sham stimulation, but late-stage whole-brain evidence needs cautious interpretation.

Why it matters for you: Directly on TMS-EEG source analysis, a method you want to learn and that fits your interest in cortical excitability.
