Psychedelics shift decision strategies and reconfigure orbitofrontal dynamics.

C Delgado-Sallent, S A Ahmed, A Khawaja-Lopez, B S Lee, U Mishra, R A Senne, S Ramirez, B B Scott
Science advances · 2026-11-20 · doi:10.1126/sciadv.aeb3041
PsilocybinKetamine / esketaminePre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#psilocybin#ketamine#orbitofrontal cortex

Aim Determine how psilocybin and ketamine reshape perceptual decision-making and orbitofrontal population dynamics in mice.

Methods Mice trained on a free-response perceptual decision task disentangling bias from sensory strategies, given acute psilocybin or ketamine, analysed with drift diffusion, GLM-HMM, whole-brain c-Fos mapping and OFC calcium imaging.

Results Both drugs slowed decisions and improved accuracy via higher decision thresholds and a shift toward sensory-engaged states. OFC population dynamics were reorganised, with preserved task selectivity but altered temporal structure and population correlations.

Conclusion Psychedelics shift decision-making toward greater reliance on sensory evidence while reshaping cortical population dynamics.

Why it matters for you: Population dynamics and behavioural modelling of psychedelic effects connect to your neurodynamics work and interest in psychedelic mechanisms.
