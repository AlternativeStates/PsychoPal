Direct evidence for psychoactive cannabis use 2800 years ago.

Gino Caspari, Timur Sadykov, Daria Fomicheva, Jegor Blochin, Dominic Armbruster, Oliver Happel, Natalia Ryabogina, Kseniia Boxleitner, Robert Spengler
Science (New York, N.Y.) · 2026-11-05 · doi:10.1126/science.aeg3800
Substance use (other)Peer reviewed

Background Cannabis is the world's most-used illicit psychoactive plant, yet direct prehistoric evidence for its consumption is rare.

Methods In this study, we present the earliest direct evidence of cannabis use: offerings of plant parts and chemical compounds in ancient human hair recovered from a monumental tomb in Siberia.

Conclusion The findings suggest that the plant was used for its psychoactive qualities across Eurasia, where specific wild populations might have had naturally higher active compound levels.
