Low-Threshold Intermittent Hypoxia Exacerbates LPS-Induced Depressive-Like Behaviors and Cognitive Deficits While Potentiating the Hippocampal STING-TBK1 Pathway.

Xiao-Yi Kong, Ling Zhang, Chang-Hao Chen, Rong-Rong Cai, Huan-Zhong Liu
Brain and behavior · 2026 · doi:10.1002/brb3.71926
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedInformation theory / complexityComputational / whole-brain modelling#neuroinflammation#hypoxia#depression

Aim Determine whether low-threshold intermittent hypoxia worsens LPS-induced depressive-like behaviour, cognitive deficits and hippocampal neuroinflammation.

Methods Adult male mice exposed to low-threshold intermittent hypoxia during systemic LPS challenge; tail suspension, forced swim and sucrose preference tests; Morris water maze; hippocampal STING-TBK1-IRF3 pathway, synaptic markers and cytokine measures.

Results Hypoxia alone did not impair behaviour, but it amplified LPS-induced depressive-like behaviour and cognitive decline. It also intensified STING-TBK1 activation, reduced PSD-95 and synaptophysin, and raised pro-inflammatory cytokines.

Conclusion Hypoxia synergises with systemic inflammation to worsen mood, cognition and neuroinflammation, possibly through hyperactive STING-dependent immune signalling.

Why it matters for you: Connects to your work on inflammation and neuroplasticity in mood disorders, though it is a preclinical rodent study.
