Morally motivated crimes are less sensitive to punishment.

Raihan Alam, Tage S Rai
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1073/pnas.2624057123
ClinicalPeer reviewed#punishment#moral motivation#recidivism

Aim Whether morally motivated crimes are less sensitive to punishment than materially motivated ones.

Methods Ten studies combining federal offender data (over 30,000 offenders), judgment experiments and economic games with general-population and offender participants.

Results Moral motives predicted higher recidivism while material motives predicted lower recidivism. Morally motivated crimes were less sensitive to punishment across crime types and consequence manipulations, and players taking money for moral reasons were less deterred by fines.

Conclusion Punishment may deter morally motivated offending less effectively, with implications for sentencing and restorative justice approaches.

Why it matters for you: Largely outside your fields; a neuroscientist might find it interesting as a psychological account of how motives modulate sensitivity to aversive consequences.
