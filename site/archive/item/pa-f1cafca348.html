A Single Dose of Psilocybin Increases Synaptic Density and Decreases 5-HT2A Receptor Density in the Pig Brain

Nakul Ravi Raval, Annette Johansen, Lene Lundgaard Donovan, Nídia Fernandez Ros, Brice Ozenne, Hanne Demant Hansen, Gitte M. Knudsen
International Journal of Molecular Sciences · 2021-01-15 · doi:10.3390/ijms22020835
PsilocybinPeer reviewedMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#psilocybin#sv2a#5-ht2a

Aim To test whether a single psilocybin dose changes synaptic density (SV2A) and 5-HT2A receptor density in the pig brain.

Methods Twenty-four awake pigs received psilocybin 0.08 mg/kg or saline intravenously and were euthanised at one or seven days. Autoradiography of hippocampus and prefrontal cortex used [3H]UCB-J, [3H]MDL100907 and [3H]Cimbi-36.

Results One day after psilocybin, hippocampal SV2A density was 4.42% higher and 5-HT2A density was lower in hippocampus and prefrontal cortex (−15.21% to −50.19%), significant for hippocampus across ligands and for prefrontal cortex with Cimbi-36 only. At seven days SV2A remained higher (+9.24% hippocampus, +6.10% prefrontal cortex), with no 5-HT2A differences.

Conclusion Psilocybin may cause persistent synaptogenesis and an acute fall in 5-HT2A density, possibly contributing to antidepressant effects.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your synaptic density PET ([11C]UCB-J) and 5-HT2A work, providing a preclinical benchmark for psilocybin plasticity.
