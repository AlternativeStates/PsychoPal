The biological basis underlying the local brain functional changes of taVNS in treating major depressive disorder: An imaging transcriptomics study.

Jifei Sun, Kai Sun, Weihui Li, Tianjiao Xu, Min Wang, Jiudong Cao, Chunchen Liu, Guolei Zhang, Limei Chen, Jiazheng Li, De Fang, Yu Xiong, Xiaoling Wang, Jun Liu, Jiliang Fang
Journal of affective disorders · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.1016/j.jad.2026.122736
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedBrain stimulationMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#taVNS#imaging transcriptomics#depression

Aim Whether the functional brain changes produced by eight weeks of taVNS in major depression map onto specific gene expression and neurotransmitter receptor patterns.

Methods Prospective single-arm longitudinal cohort of 122 patients with MDD, resting-state fMRI before and after 8 weeks of taVNS; ALFF and ReHo change correlated with HAMD-17 change, linked to Allen Human Brain Atlas gene expression via LASSO and to 32 PET receptor maps.

Results 86 participants (70.5%) met the response criterion. Gene expression predicted ReHo response better (R2 = 0.309) than ALFF response (R2 = 0.174); ALFF linked to cation channel and NMDA pathways, ReHo to microglial and interferon signalling, with the strongest receptor correlation for GABA-A density in ReHo. Without a control group, the improvement cannot be attributed to taVNS itself.

Conclusion taVNS-associated functional changes show spatial enrichment for distinct synaptic and neuroimmune pathways, offering candidate molecular correlates, though the single-arm design limits efficacy inference.

Why it matters for you: Links taVNS-related brain changes to receptor maps such as GABA-A and CB1, relevant to your PET receptor work and plasticity interests.
