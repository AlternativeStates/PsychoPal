Multi-receptor whole-brain modeling reveals affinity-driven chemoarchitectural mechanism of virtual atomoxetine action

Martí Monge-Asensio, Murat Demirtaş, Thomas A Pfeffer, Gorka Zamora‐López, Jakub Vohryzek, Yonatan Sanz-Perl, Ruud L. van den Brink, Tobias H. Donner, Morten L. Kringelbach, Gustavo Deco
Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience · 2026-11-04 · doi:10.3389/fncom.2026.1854866
Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling

Aim Here, we combined pharmacological resting-state fMRI with a whole-brain model incorporating cortical expression of adrenergic and dopaminergic receptors to investigate the effects of atomoxetine, a norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor that globally elevates catecholaminergic …

Methods Using receptor similarity, we first identified a catecholaminergic chemoarchitecture comprising frontal, posterior and temporal networks, providing the neurochemical scaffold for interpreting atomoxetine-induced brain dynamics.

Results Whole-brain modeling revealed that although individual receptor parameters did not differ between conditions, their collective organization converged toward empirical receptor binding affinities. In silico forward modeling showed that this affinity-driven redistribution gave rise to a cortical modulation pattern aligned with a frontal chemoarchitectural gradient, which was sufficient to simulate the observed dynamical and connectivity changes.

Conclusion Together, these findings suggest receptor binding affinity as a key organizing principle linking molecular receptor organization to brain dynamics, providing a model-based, biologically interpretable computational mechanism through which global catecholaminergic perturbations reshape whole-brain function.
