Stress-induced dorsal striatal dopamine release is associated with changes in functional connectivity to the prefrontal cortex in nicotine-dependent individuals-a preliminary report.

Siyan Fan, Su Jin Kim, Yasmin Zakiniaeiz, Jocelyn Hoye, Bart de Laat, Alaaddin Ibrahimy, Gelsina Stanley, Chiang-Shan R Li, Kelly P Cosgrove, Michelle Hampson, Evan D Morris
Frontiers in neuroscience · 2026-10-27 · doi:10.3389/fnins.2026.1946892
fMRINicotine / smokingClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#dopamine pet#stress#functional connectivity

Aim Whether stress-induced dopamine release in the striatum relates to changes in striatal-prefrontal functional connectivity in nicotine-dependent individuals.

Methods Within-subject multimodal study in eight smokers using endotoxin (LPS) or placebo, with [11C]raclopride PET before and after methylphenidate and seed-based resting-state fMRI.

Results LPS raised cortisol and striatal dopamine release relative to placebo (p < 0.05); cortisol increases correlated with dorsal striatal release (r = 0.708, p = 0.04). Connectivity between striatum and vmPFC and dlPFC increased (p < 0.001, FDR-corrected), with a non-linear stress relationship.

Conclusion The authors suggest stress-related frontal-striatal connectivity changes reflect adaptation, while excessive stress may disrupt circuits, though the small pilot needs replication.

Why it matters for you: Combines PET dopamine measures with resting-state connectivity, closely matching your PET methods and your interest in neurodynamics and addiction.
