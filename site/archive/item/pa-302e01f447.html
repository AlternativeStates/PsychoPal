Video-Based Automated Seizure Classification Using Multimodal Large Language Models.

Lina Zhang, Richard Jiang, Tonmoy Monsoor, Jessica N Pasqua, Colin M McCrimmon, Prateik Sinha, Kartik Sharma, Muayad Alzuabi, Victor E Morales, Hailey Miranda, Chaya Manjeshwar, Vwani Roychowdhury, Rajarshi Mazumder
Annals of clinical and translational neurology · 2026-11-20 · doi:10.1002/acn3.70574
EpilepsyClinicalPeer reviewedMachine learning / AI

Aim Accurately distinguishing between epileptic seizures (ES) and nonepileptic seizures (NES) remains a significant clinical challenge, typically requiring resource-intensive inpatient video-EEG monitoring.

Methods We analyzed 90 seizure videos (45 ES, 45 NES) from 29 patients in an epilepsy monitoring unit. Each event was labeled using expert-reviewed video-EEG and annotated for 24 clinically relevant semiological features.

Results Expert-annotated features yielded near-perfect classification using KNN (precision = 0.92, recall = 0.92, F1-score = 0.92, AUC = 0.99). Compared to expert annotations, the MLLM pipeline achieved a mean recall of 0.71, accuracy of 0.58, and F1-score of 0.51 for feature extraction.

Conclusion This approach offers a scalable, explainable tool to assist in video-based epilepsy diagnosis.
