A Pharmacological Basis for Some Bad LSD "Trips"?

David E Nichols, Gerald B Billac, Ryan C Harbit, Joel M Smith
Psychedelic medicine (New Rochelle, N.Y.) · 2026-11-12 · doi:10.1177/28314425261505140
Psychedelics (general)Psychosis / schizophreniaPre-clinicalPeer reviewedPre-clinical / invasive#lsd#dopamine d4#metabolite

Aim Tests whether a metabolite of LSD explains the delayed paranoid phase of LSD intoxication.

Methods Synthesis of 13- and 14-hydroxy-LSD and in vitro receptor testing at the human dopamine D4 receptor.

Results 13-hydroxy-LSD was about 380 times more potent than LSD at the D4 receptor (EC50 36 pM versus 14 nM).

Conclusion 13-hydroxy-LSD may contribute to the delayed dopaminergic phase of LSD action, but in vivo formation and central effects remain unknown.

Why it matters for you: Offers a receptor-level hypothesis for the delayed phase of LSD action that bears on your interest in dopaminergic and serotonergic psychedelic pharmacology.
