Intravenous ketamine for major depressive episodes in routine practice: a review of non-randomized evidence on effectiveness and safety.

Tychique T Wasolua, Roger S McIntyre, Taeho Greg Rhee
Expert review of clinical pharmacology · 2026-11-26 · doi:10.1080/17512433.2026.2768386
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Peer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#ketamine#maintenance therapy#safety

Aim To review non-randomised evidence on the effectiveness and safety of intravenous racemic ketamine for major depressive episodes in routine practice.

Methods Systematic searches of PubMed, Embase and PsycINFO to 30 September 2026; 56 non-randomised studies included.

Results Response ranged from 26% to 71% and remission from 14% to 53%, overlapping RCT estimates. Benefits often waned within weeks, attrition was high, blood pressure rises were transient, and serious adverse events and urinary and hepatic outcomes were poorly reported.

Conclusion Durability of benefit and cumulative safety of repeated IV ketamine remain uncertain; systematic safety monitoring and head-to-head and maintenance trials are needed.

Why it matters for you: Directly addresses the durability and safety questions around ketamine maintenance that your neuroplastogen research bears on.
