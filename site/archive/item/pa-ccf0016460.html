The molecular and neurochemical basis of altered intrinsic neural timescales in major depressive disorder: a multimodal imaging-transcriptomic investigation of esketamine and sertraline treatment.

Xiang Liu, Yuanzhi He, Lifeng Li, Zhanxiang Hu, Haijun Li, Lihao Wu, Liping Zhang, Zihao Su, HengRui Liu, Guojiang Wu, Dechang Peng
BMC psychiatry · 2026-08-05 · doi:10.1186/s12888-026-08477-0
Ketamine / esketamineSSRIs / antidepressantsDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyInformation theory / complexity

Background Major depressive disorder (MDD) is characterized by dysfunction in higher-order cortical regions involved in emotional and cognitive processes; however, its neurobiological basis remains unclear.

Methods A total of 57 healthy controls and 41 patients with MDD were included.

Results Compared with healthy controls, patients with MDD exhibited significantly elevated INT levels in the left and right precuneus. Following treatment, patients with MDD showed significantly increased INT in the left occipital midline region and left calcarine cortex.

Conclusion These findings support a multiscale pathophysiological framework involving brain connectivity dynamics, molecular architecture, and neurochemical regulation in MDD.
