The Effects of Methylphenidate on the EEG of Children with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder.

Adam R Clarke, Robert J Barry, Alexander T Duda, Thomas W Rout, Frances M De Blasio, Rory McCarthy, Mark Selikowitz
Clinical EEG and neuroscience · 2026-11-13 · doi:10.1177/15500594261507149
EEGSubstance use (other)ClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveform#methylphenidate#eeg spectra#adhd

Aim To determine the effects of methylphenidate alone on resting EEG spectral power in children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, including the gamma band.

Methods Fifty children with AD/HD and 50 typically developing children aged 8-12 had eyes-closed resting EEG before and 1.5 hours after 10 mg methylphenidate; absolute and relative power was computed across delta to gamma bands.

Results Unmedicated, the AD/HD group showed elevated delta and posterior theta, reduced relative alpha, and reduced global gamma compared with controls. Methylphenidate increased global beta and relative gamma power.

Conclusion Methylphenidate increased fast-wave activity, but it is unclear whether this counts as normalisation of the EEG.

Why it matters for you: Resting EEG spectral changes under a pharmacological manipulation fit your interest in EEG complexity and oscillatory markers of drug effects.
