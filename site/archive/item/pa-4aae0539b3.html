Exploring subacute effects of a DMT and harmine meditation retreat on the brain's control energy landscape

Isabella Llano, Kat Hefter, Suman S. Kulkarni, Klemens Egger, Milan Scheidegger, Diana Tamir, Danielle S. Bassett
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.07.764538
Psychedelics (general)PreprintConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modellingClinical trial / evidence synthesis#dmt#control energy#afterglow

Aim Whether a DMT and harmine meditation retreat changes the brain's control energy landscape in the subacute afterglow window.

Methods Exploratory analysis of rs-fMRI from a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (N = 39) before and after a three-day retreat, with control energy computed on a group structural connectome under two timescale approaches.

Results No global shift in control energy between transitions, though mean control energy rose in both groups under the long-timescale approach. The central executive network showed a post-retreat increase in the verum group surviving FDR correction, and the visual network decreased under the short-timescale approach.

Conclusion The authors conclude effects are network- and timescale-specific rather than global, but small, heterogeneous and needing confirmation in larger samples.

Why it matters for you: Applies network control theory to psychedelic afterglow effects, connecting to your neurodynamics and whole-brain modelling interests.
