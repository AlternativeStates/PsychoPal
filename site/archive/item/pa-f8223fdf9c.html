Psychedelic Use and Mental Health Diagnosis Shape Attitudes Toward Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy

Sydney Rosinsky, John Benjamin Hellier, Megan Day
RVU Elevate · 2026-11-03 · doi:10.82451/k51387
PsilocybinDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Substance use (other)PTSDClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#psychedelics#attitudes#survey

Aim Whether prior psychedelic use and mental health diagnosis are associated with attitudes towards psychedelic-assisted therapy.

Methods Cross-sectional anonymous online convenience survey of 99 adults aged 21 or older recruited via social media and text messages; t tests and Fisher exact tests comparing groups by prior psychedelic use, diagnosis and healthcare-worker status.

Results Prior users reported greater willingness to recommend psychedelic-assisted therapy (mean 3.65 vs 2.40) and greater support for therapeutic legalisation. Respondents with a diagnosis reported greater willingness to recommend it (mean 3.88 vs 2.02); interest in alternatives to daily medication did not differ, and healthcare-worker status was unrelated.

Conclusion Prior use and diagnosis were associated with greater willingness to recommend therapy; the small non-random sample limits generalisability and precludes causal inference.

Why it matters for you: Public and clinician attitudes shape the uptake of psychedelic therapies you study, though the sample is small and self-selected.
