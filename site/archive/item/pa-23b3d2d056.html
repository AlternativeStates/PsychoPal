Music illuminates sociocultural dimensions of autobiographical memory.

Cara Turnbull, Benjamin M Kubit, Kelly Jakubowski, Elizabeth H Margulis
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.1073/pnas.2536885123
Peer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive coding

Background Researchers have long theorized that autobiographical memory relies on sociocultural structures, such as life scripts and event schemas.

Methods We collected free-response descriptions of autobiographical memories evoked by short clips of familiar songs from university undergraduates (n = 233).

Results Memories cued by songs from the same category displayed striking similarities, suggesting that particular types of music cue memories that are similar between listeners but that vary according to characteristics such as music genre and release date.
