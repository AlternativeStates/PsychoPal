Longitudinal diffusion tensor imaging identifies coordinated white matter remodeling during cocaine self-administration in mice.

Zachary Fernandez, Alice McQueney, Melinda Osowski, Xiang Liu, Yun Liang, Shuangtao Ma, Andrew Bender, Norman Scheel, Erik Shapiro, Alfred J Robison, Chunqi Qian
bioRxiv : the preprint server for biology · 2026-10-20 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.14.759212
MRI (structural/diffusion)Substance use (other)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingPreprocessing / source / toolsPre-clinical / invasiveMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)

Background White matter microstructural alterations are increasingly recognized as a feature of cocaine use disorder (CUD), yet the longitudinal progression and biological underpinnings of these changes remain poorly understood.

Methods Although rodent intravenous self-administration (IVSA) provides a translational model of voluntary cocaine use, longitudinal diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) has not previously been combined with cocaine IVSA in mice.

Conclusion This framework provides a translational platform for future studies integrating longitudinal neuroimaging with genetically modified mouse models, tractography, and histological validation to investigate the mechanisms underlying cocaine-associated white matter plasticity.
