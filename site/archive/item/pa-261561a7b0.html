Cortical Hypoperfusion in Acute Anorexia Nervosa Is Graded Along the Sensorimotor-Association Hierarchy.

Enrico Collantoni, Massimiliano Facca, Sofia Gentili, Giovanni Librizzi, Renzo Manara, Angela Favaro, Valentina Meregalli
The International journal of eating disorders · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1002/eat.70278
MRI (structural/diffusion)Opioid use disorderEating disordersHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#anorexia nervosa#asl perfusion#functional gradient

Aim To determine whether cortical hypoperfusion in acute anorexia nervosa is uniform or organised along the cortex's principal functional gradient, and whether it dissociates from cortical thinning.

Methods 44 acutely underweight women with anorexia nervosa and 45 healthy women underwent 3T pseudo-continuous arterial spin labelling with partial-volume correction, analysed across the Schaefer 100-region atlas and compared with cortical thickness and 18 PET/SPECT receptor maps.

Results Perfusion was lower in 97 of 100 regions, maximal in orbitofrontal and prefrontal cortex (composite d = -0.56). The deficit scaled with position along the principal functional gradient (ρ = -0.56), and hypoperfusion was greater where D1, CB1, mGluR5, M1, 5-HT2A and μ-opioid systems are dense.

Conclusion Cortical hypoperfusion in acute anorexia nervosa is graded along the sensorimotor-association axis, dissociable from thinning and aligned with reward-related neurochemistry. The cross-sectional design limits causal interpretation.

Why it matters for you: Combines cerebral blood flow, the principal functional gradient and receptor maps, closely matching your neurodynamics and PET neurochemistry interests.
