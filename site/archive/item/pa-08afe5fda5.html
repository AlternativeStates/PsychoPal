UNC13A splice correction rescues synaptic dysfunction induced by TDP-43 loss.

Matthew J Keuss, Peter Harley, Eugeni Ryadnov, Rachel E Jackson, Moritz Boll, Matteo Zanovello, Oscar G Wilkins, Simone Barattucci, Dragana Ilic, Rebecca L Simkin, Max Z Y J Chien, Alexander J Cammack, Darija Šoltić, Puja R Mehta, Yan To Ling, Maria Kowald, Ryan D Morrie, Georgiana Miller, Marcio G Oliveira, Graham Duddy, Sophie Wood, Joanna E Parkes, Aparna Sinha, Andrés F Correa-Sánchez, Adrian M Isaacs, Peter L Oliver, Fursham Hamid, Eric M Green, Elizabeth M C Fisher, Benjamin Davies, Giampietro Schiavo, Mala Shah, Noa Lipstein, Juan Burrone, Pietro Fratta
Nature · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.1038/s41586-026-11256-2
Dementia / Alzheimer'sClinicalPeer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive codingConnectivity / synchrony

Background TDP-43 nuclear depletion is a defining pathological feature of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and frontotemporal dementia that induces widespread cryptic splicing defects in hundreds of transcripts1-8.

Methods Here we show that TDP-43 loss induces a predegenerative state in which neurons remain structurally intact but suffer a substantial breakdown in synaptic communication.

Results In human neurons this intervention restored spontaneous and evoked synaptic vesicle release, release probability at individual synapses and wider network synchrony.

Conclusion Together, this establishes UNC13A splice correction as a mechanistically defined and genetically validated therapeutic strategy relevant to roughly 97% of patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and roughly 50% of patients with frontotemporal dementia …
