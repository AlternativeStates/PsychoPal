Dynamic epigenetic changes during antidepressant pharmacotherapy in major depressive disorder.

Svenja Müller, Josef Frank, Eric Zillich, Fabian Streit, Valentin Nicolai Koch, Miriam A Schiele, Katharina Domschke, Andre Tadic, David P Herzog, Stefanie Wagner, Marianne B Müller, Giulia Treccani, Nils C Gassen, Marcella Rietschel, Stephanie H Witt, Klaus Lieb, Jan Engelmann, Lea Zillich
Molecular psychiatry · 2026-11-02 · doi:10.1038/s41380-026-03945-1
Depression (incl. TRD/MDD)Peer reviewed

Background Although antidepressants remain the main pharmacological treatment for major depressive disorder (MDD), therapeutic response varies, highlighting the need for molecular markers that predict treatment response, as well as insights into the biological …

Methods To date, no longitudinal EWAS has examined methylation trajectories across multiple time points during antidepressant treatment in MDD.

Results Baseline methylation levels were associated with depressive symptom severity at day 28 and day 56 through two and six DMRs, respectively (e.g., TNRC6C, CAT), whereas no single DMP reached significance. Longitudinal analyses identified one DMP associated with improvement in depressive symptoms (YLMP1) and three DMRs showing methylation changes over time (e.g., GPR126, PM20D1).

Conclusion This study provides first longitudinal evidence of regionally coordinated DNA methylation changes during antidepressant pharmacotherapy, revealing alterations in genes involved in neuroplasticity and inflammatory processes that are associated with clinical response.
