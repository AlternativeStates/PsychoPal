A shared slowing axis and phenotype-specific rhythms link clinical EEG to FDG-PET dementia phenotypes

N. Satyadev, V. Gogineni, N. Corriveau-Lecavalier, G. Zhang, B. Brinkmann, Y. Varatharajah, G. S. Day, J. L. Whitwell, K. A. Josephs, K. Kantarci, B. F. Boeve, R. C. Petersen, M. Breakspear, V. J. Lowe, D. T. Jones
biorxiv (neuroscience) · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.09.764237
Dementia / Alzheimer'sClinicalPreprintSpectral / aperiodic / waveformPreprocessing / source / toolsMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#eeg#fdg-pet#dementia phenotypes

Aim Whether data-driven factors from clinical EEG differ across FDG-PET-defined neurodegenerative dementia phenotypes.

Methods 455 patients with EEG and FDG-PET within one year; six EEG factors from nonnegative tensor decomposition compared across 14 StateViewer diagnostic classes, adjusted for a slowing index and related to regional metabolism and cognition (n = 98).

Results Four oscillatory factors varied across phenotypes, most strongly in dementia with Lewy bodies, dysexecutive Alzheimer disease and posterior cortical atrophy. Slowing explained much of the alpha and beta effects, while posterior theta-slow alpha stayed phenotype-specific after adjustment.

Conclusion The authors conclude decomposed clinical EEG separates oscillatory activity from artefact in ways tracking metabolic phenotype, supporting scalable EEG-based dementia phenotyping, though frontotemporal phenotypes were poorly discriminated.

Why it matters for you: Connects EEG spectral decomposition to PET metabolic phenotypes, bridging your EEG and PET interests.
