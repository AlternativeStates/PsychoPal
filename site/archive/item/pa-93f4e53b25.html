2-AG as a potential biomarker for predicting response to short-term fluoxetine treatment in a mouse model of depression.

Andrea Rodríguez-López, El Chérif Ibrahim, Victor Gorgievski, Miquel Martin, Patricia Robledo, Rafael Maldonado, Eleni Tzavara, Beltrán Álvarez-Pérez
Translational psychiatry · 2026-11-13 · doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04548-8
SSRIs / antidepressantsDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Pre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling

Background Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a complex psychiatric condition with significant individual and social impact and marked variability in treatment response.

Methods Fluoxetine, one of the most commonly prescribed antidepressants, influences not only serotonergic signaling but also the endocannabinoid system.

Results Liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry analyses of hippocampal and prefrontal cortex samples revealed that behavioral improvement following short-term fluoxetine treatment was accompanied by increased hippocampal 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG), along with reduced GABA and glutamate levels in the prefrontal cortex.

Conclusion These findings suggest that increased 2-AG levels may predict early response to fluoxetine.
