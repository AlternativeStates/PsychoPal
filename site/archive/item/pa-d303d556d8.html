Heme signatures in mineralized red blood cells within vascular canals of a 115-million-year-old dinosaur bone

Kliti Grice, Yuta Isaji, Jasmina Wiemann, Paul F. Greenwood, Madison Tripp, Maria A. Diaz-Mateus, Stephen F. Poropat, Xiao Sun, Calum P. Fox, Luke M. Brosnan, Renan Alfredo Machado Bantim, Lorenz Schwark, Pieter T. Visscher, Nanako O. Ogawa, Julien Demore, Antônio A. F. Saraiva, William D.A. Rickard, Naohiko Ohkouchi, Juliana Manso Sayão, Alex I. Holman, Peter Hopper, Robert H.C. Madden, Aaron C. Dodd, Victor O. Leshyk, Hossein Rahimpour-Bonab, Arthur Vicentini de Oliveira, Sebastian Stanley, Lisa Smith, Bree Morgan, Alexander W. A. Kellner
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · 2026-11-02 · doi:10.1073/pnas.2614248123
Peer reviewed

Background Detection of endogenous biomolecules in vertebrate fossils remains controversial because labile compounds degrade quickly and are highly susceptible to contamination.

Methods Here, we present data on the preservation of heme-derived tetrapyrroles, and vascular canal-constrained ellipsoidal microstructures, inferred to be red blood cells, in limb bones of Aratasaurus museunacionali , a 115-million-year-old theropod dinosaur.

Results Separate spatially resolved analyses by Raman spectroscopy and transmission electron microscopy confirmed the ellipsoid microstructure localization of i) porphyrin signals of heme and ii) key elements of heme (Fe, N, and C) and mineral stabilizing hausmannite (Mn 3 O 4 ), respectively.

Conclusion These results demonstrate blood-derived biomolecules in mineralized red blood cells of a dinosaur.
