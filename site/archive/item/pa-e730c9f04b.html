Neuroprotective activity of sub-anesthetic ketamine in a preclinical model of Parkinson's disease is not blocked by antagonizing brain-derived neurotrophic factor signaling.

Carolyn J Stopera, Mitchell J Bartlett, Kelsey Bernard, Rachel Conklin, Sansita Singh, Jennifer B Frye, Lalitha Madhavan, Kristian R Doyle, Scott J Sherman, Kathy Steece-Collier, Torsten Falk
bioRxiv : the preprint server for biology · 2026-10-29 · doi:10.64898/2026.10.28.762430
Ketamine / esketamineParkinson'sPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingPre-clinical / invasive

Aim In this report, we investigated if sub-anesthetic ketamine is also neuroprotective in a progressive 6-OHDA model of PD via neurotrophic action through the tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB) receptor.

Results Semi-quantitative western analysis showed that the relative tyrosine hydroxylase content in the striatum was not significantly increased in ketamine-treated rats compared to vehicle.
