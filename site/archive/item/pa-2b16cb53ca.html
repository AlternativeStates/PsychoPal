Repeated prefrontal transcranial direct current stimulation improves behavioral regulation and modulates resting-state EEG microstate dynamics in young adults with problematic smartphone use: a randomized sham-controlled longitudinal study.

Jun Chen, Jianming Cheng, Jinghua Teng, Hui Liu, Bo Yang, Yanxia Zhu, Enming Zhang, Fan Cheng, Yingying Zeng
Frontiers in psychiatry · 2026-10-19 · doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1928163
EEGClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyBrain stimulation

Aim This study investigated whether repeated prefrontal tDCS improves behavioral outcomes and reorganizes resting-state electroencephalographic (EEG) microstate dynamics in young adults with PSU.

Methods Eighty participants meeting validated criteria for PSU were randomly assigned to receive active (n = 40) or sham (n = 40) tDCS.

Results Compared with sham stimulation, active tDCS significantly reduced objectively measured smartphone use, craving intensity, and functional impairment while improving behavioral regulation. During eyes-closed recording, microstate C occurrence decreased whereas microstate D occurrence increased significantly.

Conclusion These findings provide preliminary evidence that repeated prefrontal tDCS is associated with concurrent behavioral changes and alterations in resting-state EEG microstate dynamics in young adults with PSU.
