Acute Effects of Prolonged Intermittent Theta Burst Stimulation on Cortical Excitability: A Randomized Crossover TMS-EEG Study

Sharie Xiao Wang, Wanda Man Wai Chau, Vera Wai Man Lam, Tim T Z Lin, Roy Rongyue Zeng, Penny Ping Qin, Minxia Jin, Adam Weili Xia, Jack Jiaqi Zhang, Tifei Yuan, Yoshihiro Noda, Georg S. Kranz
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-27 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.27.769602
TMS / TMS-EEGHealthy volunteersClinicalPreprintSpectral / aperiodic / waveformERP / MMN / predictive codingBrain stimulationClinical trial / evidence synthesis#tms-eeg#theta burst#eeg

Aim Whether a single session of prolonged intermittent theta burst stimulation changes cortical oscillations and TMS-evoked potentials in healthy males.

Methods Randomised sham-controlled crossover trial in 40 healthy males receiving active or sham piTBS over left DLPFC, with inter-train EEG analysed in 37 participants and TMS-evoked potentials, working memory and attention measured in all 40.

Results Left frontal theta power followed different trajectories across blocks under active versus sham stimulation (interaction p = .014). No TEP clusters differed between conditions; exploratory analyses showed frontal N100 decrease and posterior N100 increase within active piTBS. Behavioural outcomes did not change.

Conclusion A single active piTBS session modulated frontal theta between trains; whether this relates to cumulative plasticity or antidepressant outcomes remains to be tested.

Why it matters for you: Combines TMS-EEG with an acute plasticity-relevant stimulation protocol, closely matching your TMS-EEG and EEG-based excitability interests.
