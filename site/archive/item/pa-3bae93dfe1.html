Multimodal PET/MR imaging in Alzheimer's disease: A comparative study of structural, metabolic, synaptic and perfusion measures.

Charles Carron, Greet Vanderlinden, Mathieu Vandenbulcke, Rik Vandenberghe, Koen Van Laere
European journal of nuclear medicine and molecular imaging · 2026-11-23 · doi:10.1007/s00259-026-08235-0
MRI (structural/diffusion)Dementia / Alzheimer'sHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedInformation theory / complexityMachine learning / AIMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#sv2a pet#pet-mr#alzheimer's disease

Aim To compare structural, metabolic, synaptic and perfusion PET/MR measures for detecting neurodegeneration and discriminating Alzheimer's disease from healthy volunteers.

Methods 36 biomarker-confirmed Alzheimer's patients underwent 18F-FDG, 18F-SynVesT-1 (SV2A) PET/MR and neuropsychological testing, with cortical thickness, ASL perfusion and volumetry compared against a healthy cohort using cross-validated classification.

Results 18F-FDG PET showed large reductions and the best discrimination (AUC 0.94). Cortical thickness, SV2A PET and volumetry gave similar performance (AUC 0.81-0.86), while ASL was weakest (AUC 0.72) and absent in MCI.

Conclusion Multimodal measures show overlapping but distinct patterns, with synaptic and structural abnormalities present in MCI and perfusion deficits emerging only in dementia-stage disease.

Why it matters for you: Directly applies SV2A PET and PET-MR methods you use, benchmarking synaptic density imaging against established biomarkers.
