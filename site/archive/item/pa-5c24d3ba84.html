Psilocybin desynchronizes the human brain

Joshua S. Siegel, Subha Subramanian, Demetrius Perry, Benjamin P. Kay, Evan M. Gordon, Timothy O. Laumann, Travis R. Reneau, Nicholas V. Metcalf, Ravi V. Chacko, Caterina Gratton, Christine Horan, Samuel R. Krimmel, Joshua S. Shimony, Julia A. Schweiger, Dean Foster Wong, David A. Bender, Kristen M. Scheidter, Forrest I. Whiting, Jonah A. Raichle, Abraham Z. Snyder, Ginger E. Nicol, Nico U.F. Dosenbach

Aim To determine how psilocybin reshapes large-scale human brain functional connectivity and whether these changes relate to the subjective experience and lasting effects.

Methods Longitudinal precision fMRI with roughly 18 scans per participant in healthy adults, before, during and for three weeks after 25 mg psilocybin or 40 mg methylphenidate, with a further psilocybin dose 6-12 months later.

Results Psilocybin produced over threefold greater acute connectivity change than methylphenidate, driven by cross-scale desynchronisation, strongest in the default mode network. Anterior hippocampus to default mode connectivity stayed reduced for weeks.

Conclusion Persistent hippocampal-default mode reduction may be a neuroanatomical correlate of the proplasticity and therapeutic effects of psychedelics. The sample is small and healthy-volunteer based.

Why it matters for you: Core to your interest in psychedelics as neuroplastogens and to the default-mode and hippocampal network changes you measure.
