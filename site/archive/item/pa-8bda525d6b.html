Difference in Patterns of Polysubstance Use Between Adults With Primary Cocaine Versus Methamphetamine Use Disorder: Findings From NIDA CTN-0108 Study.

Zahraa Atoui, Jenna L McCauley, Ralph Ward, Manish Kumar Jha, Karen J Hartwell, Russell Toll, Susan Sonne, Brenda Brunner-Jackson, Geetha A Subramaniam, Madhukar H Trivedi, Kathleen Brady
Substance use & addiction journal · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1177/29767342261495692
TMS / TMS-EEGAlcohol use disorderSubstance use (other)ClinicalPeer reviewedBrain stimulationClinical trial / evidence synthesis#cocaine#methamphetamine#polysubstance use

Aim How demographics and polysubstance use patterns differ between people with primary cocaine and methamphetamine use disorders screened for a TMS trial.

Methods Screening data from 320 participants in NIDA CTN-0108 (120 with cocaine use disorder, 200 with methamphetamine use disorder), comparing demographics and substance use patterns.

Results Alcohol use was more prevalent in the cocaine group (67.9% vs 50%, p = 0.048) and cannabis use was similar (50.9% vs 46.1%). Polysubstance use remained common despite exclusion of moderate-to-severe comorbid SUDs.

Conclusion Polysubstance use persists in stimulant use disorder even under strict trial criteria, indicating a complex clinical profile that trial design should account for.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to trial design for stimulant use disorder and to the clinical populations your psychedelic and neuromodulation work may later target.
