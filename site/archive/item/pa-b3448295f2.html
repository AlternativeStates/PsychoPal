Transcriptional profiling of antidepressant ketamine and electroconvulsive therapy treatment.

Artemis Zavaliangos-Petropulu, Ginny Ghang, Toni Boltz, Paloma Pfeiffer, Lingyu Zhan, Brandon Taraku, Eliza Congdon, Randall T Espinoza, Katherine L Narr, Roel A Ophoff
Translational psychiatry · 2026-08-05 · doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04196-y
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchrony#ketamine#ect#blood transcriptomics

Aim To characterise longitudinal peripheral blood transcriptional changes during ECT and serial ketamine treatment in treatment-resistant depression.

Methods Peripheral blood RNA-seq in 37 ECT patients, 60 ketamine patients and 35 controls at multiple time points; differential expression and WGCNA co-expression network analysis with multiple-comparison correction.

Results No longitudinal changes were detected for individual genes or networks after FDR correction. Baseline comparisons showed immune-related enrichment and one gene (IGKV1-9) differing between ketamine remitters and non-remitters, and several modules differing between ECT patients and controls.

Conclusion Despite clinical improvement, peripheral transcriptional change was limited; pre-treatment differences hint at predictive value, and larger samples are needed.

Why it matters for you: Speaks to biomarkers of ketamine response in TRD, a question central to your ketamine and plasticity work, though the peripheral signal is weak.
