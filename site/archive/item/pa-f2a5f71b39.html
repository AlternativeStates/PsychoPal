Psychedelics and Sleep: Preclinical and Human Studies-A Systematic Review.

Kim Supit, Giordano N Rossi, José Augusto S Reis, Leman H Dursun, Serdar M Dursun, Jaime E C Hallak, Rafael G Dos Santos
Psychiatry and clinical psychopharmacology · 2026-10-11 · doi:10.65801/pcp.3606
PsilocybinDMTAyahuascaPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis#psychedelics#sleep#systematic review

Aim To review preclinical and human evidence on how classic psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin, DMT/ayahuasca) alter sleep architecture, sleep quality, EEG activity and dreams.

Methods PRISMA-guided systematic review of PubMed up to May 2026; twelve eligible preclinical and clinical studies, with validity assessed by appropriate quality tools.

Results Preclinical studies consistently showed acute REM suppression, delayed REM onset and increased wakefulness. Human findings were heterogeneous: psilocybin prolonged REM latency, ayahuasca reduced REM duration, and slow-wave activity results were mixed.

Conclusion Classic psychedelics cause acute but transient sleep changes, mainly in REM sleep. Whether these contribute to therapeutic effects is uncertain, and the review was not prospectively registered.

Why it matters for you: Your work on psychedelic clinical pharmacology and EEG markers makes the REM and slow-wave findings directly relevant to psilocybin and DMT studies.
