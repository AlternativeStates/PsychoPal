The Multi-Objective Computational Design and Prioritization of Novel TSPO PET Radioligands for Improved Inflammation Imaging after Traumatic Brain Injury

Dhruv Subramanian
ChemRxiv · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.26434/chemrxiv.15003660/v2
PETPre-clinicalPreprintInformation theory / complexityComputational / whole-brain modellingMachine learning / AIMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#tspo#pet radioligand#molecular dynamics

Aim Design and prioritise novel TSPO PET radioligands with improved physicochemistry for imaging neuroinflammation after traumatic brain injury.

Methods Cheminformatic pipeline benchmarking eight second-generation ligands, generating a 1,922-analogue haloalkylated dataset from emapunil, then PBPK modelling, plasma-input Logan analysis, ADMET, molecular dynamics and retrosynthesis.

Results Analogue A7 showed a 5.4-fold higher partition coefficient and fourfold higher brain-plasma ratio than the reference in PBPK simulations, with stable Logan estimates and significantly more pocket contacts in molecular dynamics. It was the only non-dominated candidate in Pareto analysis, although one synthetic terminal remained unresolved.

Conclusion A7 is the strongest candidate, demonstrating the value of a cohesive cheminformatic pipeline for PET radioligand development, though synthesis remains to be confirmed.

Why it matters for you: Combines PET radioligand development with molecular dynamics, matching your computational chemistry and PET imaging interests.
