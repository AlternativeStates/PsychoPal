Sample size critically shapes the reliability of EEG case-control findings in psychiatry.

Aida Ebadi, Sara Kaderi, Borja Rodríguez-Herreros, Nadia Chabane, Sahar Allouch, Mahmoud Hassan
Imaging neuroscience (Cambridge, Mass.) · 2026-11-17 · doi:10.1162/imag.a.1453
EEGAnxietyClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformInformation theory / complexity#eeg complexity#sample size#psychiatry

Aim Quantify how sample size affects the reliability of EEG case-control findings across psychiatric conditions in children and adolescents.

Methods Resting-state EEG from 2,874 participants aged 5–18 years, including ADHD, autism, anxiety, learning disorders and controls, with spectral, temporal and complexity features compared across repeated random subsamples of varying size.

Results Small samples gave unstable, inflated and highly variable effect sizes, while larger samples converged on consistent but small effects. Statistical power rose steeply with sample size, while false positive rates stayed relatively stable.

Conclusion Sample size critically shapes EEG case-control outcomes, and conventional case-control designs may be limited for biomarker discovery in psychiatry.

Why it matters for you: Directly addresses the reliability of EEG complexity measures in psychiatric case-control designs, central to your EEG complexity and computational psychiatry work.
