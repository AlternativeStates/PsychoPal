Ketamine, an interoceptive associative agent? Evidence from ketamine-assisted Mindfulness-Oriented Recovery Enhancement for opioid use disorder.

Benjamin R Lewis, Eric L Garland
Journal of psychopharmacology (Oxford, England) · 2026-11-05 · doi:10.1177/02698811261495546
Ketamine / esketamineOpioid use disorderClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling

Aim This secondary analysis examined whether ketamine-assisted psychotherapy (KAP) added to Mindfulness-Oriented Recovery Enhancement (MORE) improved interoceptive awareness and whether these changes mediated reductions in opioid craving.

Methods Sixty-eight adults with OUD receiving buprenorphine were randomized to 8 weeks of MORE with or without KAP. Posttreatment EMA data were available for 18 MORE + KAP and 15 MORE participants.

Results Piecewise modeling showed a greater increase in EMA-assessed body awareness salience in MORE + KAP during days 1–35, spanning the pre-dosing and first-ketamine-dose period ( B = 0.039, standard error (SE) = 0.005, p < 0.001).

Conclusion Adjunctive ketamine was associated with rapid, sustained increases in body awareness salience.
