Neural and Experiential Dynamics of High-Ventilation Breathwork Mirror Psychedelic States

Lana Porter, Ece Kaya, Kamlesh Chalise, Janina Klarmann, Armin Goffin, Muad Y Abd El Hay, Nina Harrter, Borja Rodríguez-de-Santiago, Joshua Neil Hindmarsh, Naman Jain, Abdellatif Nemri, Barbara Breitenstein, Christopher Timmermann, Marieke Louise Schölvinck, Isabel Dziobek, Martha Nari Havenith
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-13 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.11.765965
Psychedelics (general)PreprintSpectral / aperiodic / waveformERP / MMN / predictive codingConnectivity / synchronyInformation theory / complexityNaturalistic paradigms#breathwork#eeg complexity#psychedelic-like states

Aim Whether the neural dynamics evoked by high-ventilation breathwork resemble those of classical psychedelic states, and whether they track subjective experience depth.

Methods 64-channel EEG in 25 experienced breathwork practitioners, with resting-state recordings interleaved through a naturalistic high-ventilation breathwork session; experience depth and content also recorded.

Results Breathwork produced broadband cortical desynchronisation, higher signal complexity, reduced alpha power and flattened cortical hierarchy, with these effects correlating with subjective depth at minute resolution. The effects were not explained by music listening or active interoception.

Conclusion The authors argue these are shared neural features of psychedelic-like states regardless of induction method, so breathwork may access psychedelic-like cortical states without drugs.

Why it matters for you: Directly relevant to your EEG complexity and criticality work, and to brain–body coupling via breath, as a non-pharmacological route to psychedelic-like cortical dynamics.
