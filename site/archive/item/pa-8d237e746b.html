OPM-MEG is characterized by increased sensitivity for visual gamma-band oscillations: A parallel EEG/OPM-MEG study

Darío Gordillo, Marion Brickwedde, Paul Anders, Peter Krüger, Tilmann H. Sander, Peter J. Uhlhaas
Imaging Neuroscience · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.1162/imag.a.1484
MEGClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformConnectivity / synchronyClinical trial / evidence synthesis#opm-meg#gamma oscillations#eeg

Aim Whether OPM-MEG detects visually induced gamma-band oscillations with higher sensitivity than EEG.

Methods Simultaneous EEG and OPM-MEG recordings in 26 participants during a visual grating task; gamma (30–80 Hz) and alpha/beta power and inter-trial phase coherence compared across modalities, with effect sizes as Cohen's dz.

Results OPM-MEG showed significantly higher SNR for visually induced gamma responses than EEG (dz = 0.97), seen in most participants with higher split-half correlations (EEG 0.88; OPM-MEG 0.94). Alpha/beta changes and ITPC showed no consistent modality difference.

Conclusion OPM-MEG offers enhanced sensitivity for visual gamma oscillations relative to EEG, which may support more precise study of cortical circuit function.

Why it matters for you: Bears on your interest in EEG and MEG methods and in gamma-band dynamics for neurodynamics work.
