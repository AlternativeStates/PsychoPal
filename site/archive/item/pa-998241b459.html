Reference-Region Normalization Outperforms Data-Driven Approaches in [18F]FDG PET of Dementia with Lewy Bodies.

Antoine Rogeau, Fiene Marie Kuijper, Romain Delsanti, Théophile Bieth, Dario Saracino, Raffaella Migliaccio, Fabien Hauw, Marc Teichmann, Stéphanie Bombois, Nadya Pyatigorskaya, Marine Soret, Thibaud Charrier, Marie Chupin, François-Xavier Lejeune, Hugo Boniface, Nicolas Villain, Aurélie Kas
NeuroImage · 2026-11-19 · doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.122359
Dementia / Alzheimer'sHealthy volunteersPre-clinicalPeer reviewedMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)

Background Data-driven intensity normalization has recently emerged as an alternative to proportional scaling (PS) and reference-region (RR) for brain [18F]FDG PET analysis.

Methods For each scaling factor, we measured (i) their normative alignment between DLB and HC distributions; (ii) disease independence via correlation with a DLB meta-ROI; (iii) interindividual stability using coefficients of variation; and (iv) temporal …

Results RR normalization yielded smaller DLB-HC differences in scaling factors, weaker correlations with the DLB meta-ROI, reduced coefficients of variation, and lower rates of change compared with PS, iPS, HN, and iHN. The simulation study showed that larger scaling-factor mean differences between patients and controls, and higher coefficients of variation, were associated with greater recovery error.

Conclusion Our findings support RR-based normalization as the preferred standard in DLB, outperforming PS, HN and data-driven approaches.
