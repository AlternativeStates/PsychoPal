Poisoned hunting weapons, megafauna, and plant use at a 125,000-year-old Arabian paleolakeshore.

Chris Clarkson, Gilbert J Price, Amir Kalifi, Giacomo Losi, Carney Matheson, Faisal Al-Jibreen, Abdullah M Alsharekh, Simon J Armitage, Ikram Blilou, Nicole Boivin, Paul S Breeze, Nick Drake, Mathieu Duval, Sophia Gradwell, Huw S Groucutt, Maria Guagnin, Juliana Guzmán, Laurence Hapiot, Virginia Martínez-Pillado, Jihede Haj Messaoud, Kasih Norman, Veerle Rots, Ian H Sobbe, Mathew Stewart, Michael D Petraglia, Frans van Buchem
Science (New York, N.Y.) · 2026-11-12 · doi:10.1126/science.aef9342
Peer reviewed

Background After the African origin of Homo sapiens, multiple dispersals radiated into Asia, yet the Saharo-Arabian archaeological record remains sparse.

Methods In this study, we report on 'Uruq Aljelibah, a Middle Paleolithic site dated to ~125,000 years ago (marine isotope stage 5e) and located on the shore of Mundafan paleolake, Saudi Arabia.

Results Several points exhibit impact damage and poison residues (from Acokanthera schimperi), indicating use as hafted weapon tips within composite hunting systems.

Conclusion Megafaunal remains include hippopotamus, buffalo, gazelle, ostrich, and camel, illuminating hominin use of freshwater and grassland ecosystems.
