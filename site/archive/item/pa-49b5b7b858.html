Functional and structural neuroplasticity of motor system in hemiplegic cerebral palsy.

Sadra Shahdadian, Yanlong Song, Alireza Vaysi, Regan Christian, Madhan Bosemani, Fernando Acosta, Brooke Kimbrell, Warren Marks, Christos Papadelis
medRxiv : the preprint server for health sciences · 2026-11-20 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.20.26368423
MRI (structural/diffusion)ClinicalPeer reviewedSpectral / aperiodic / waveformBrain stimulationPreprocessing / source / toolsMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)#cerebral palsy#tms#eeg oscillations

Aim To characterise structural and functional reorganisation of the motor system in hemiplegic cerebral palsy and its links to lesion characteristics and upper-limb performance.

Methods 26 children with hemiplegic cerebral palsy and 26 typically developing controls; electromagnetic source imaging of movement-related oscillations, navigated TMS for corticospinal excitability and lateralisation, and functionally guided diffusion MRI; Melbourne Assessment 2 and correlation, latent-variable and mediation analyses.

Results Descending and commissural fibres showed lower fractional anisotropy and higher diffusivity. TMS identified ipsilateral corticospinal projections from the less affected hemisphere in 17 of 22 children. Larger lesions were associated with poorer motor performance (Rho -0.74 to -0.57).

Conclusion The authors suggest developmental neuroplasticity and reorganisation of motor pathways contribute to variable motor outcomes despite similar lesions. The study is cross-sectional and the preprint is not yet peer reviewed.

Why it matters for you: A multimodal design combining EEG oscillations, TMS and diffusion MRI, matching the methods you want to learn and use.
