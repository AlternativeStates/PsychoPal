Working memory neural correlates after ketamine in treatment-resistant depression: a randomized placebo-controlled functional magnetic resonance imaging study.

Adam Fijtman, Níall Lally, Jennifer Evans, Gregory H Jones, Jonathan P Roiser, Carlos A Zarate
Psychiatry research. Neuroimaging · 2026-11-03 · doi:10.1016/j.pscychresns.2026.112367
Ketamine / esketaminefMRIDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedClinical trial / evidence synthesis

Aim This secondary analysis investigated behavioral and neural changes during a working memory task post-ketamine infusion in TRD participants versus healthy volunteers (HVs).

Methods Data were previously collected from a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial of 22 unmedicated TRD participants (14F/8 M) and 19 matched HVs (12F/7 M). Participants received ketamine (0.5mg/kg) and placebo two weeks apart.

Results Ketamine significantly reduced Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale scores compared to placebo (p < 0.01). In region of interest (ROI) analyses, TRD participants showed reduced activation in the superior temporal gyrus versus HVs (p < 0.01) after both infusions, and both groups demonstrated decreased parieto-occipital junction activation post-ketamine versus post-placebo (p = 0.01).

Conclusion Ketamine's antidepressant effects occurred without significant changes in working memory performance, though region-specific task-related brain changes were observed.
