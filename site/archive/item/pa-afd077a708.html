Testing an accelerated transcranial magnetic stimulation protocol for methamphetamine use disorder — study protocol

David Ramírez, Jody Tanabe, Susan K. Mikulich‐Gilbertson, Drew Winters, Elizabeth Fenstermacher, Kristen M. Raymond, Hannah Gebhardt, Joseph T. Sakai
Addiction Science & Clinical Practice · 2026-11-13 · doi:10.1186/s13722-026-00742-7
TMS / TMS-EEGSubstance use (other)ClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain stimulation#itbs#methamphetamine#tms

Aim Whether an accelerated iTBS protocol over the left DLPFC is safe, feasible and shows preliminary efficacy in methamphetamine use disorder.

Methods Single-site, randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled pilot planned for 20 adults with moderate-to-severe MUD, receiving up to 40 iTBS or sham sessions over five inpatient days, then weekly follow-up for 12 weeks.

Results Not reported; this is a study protocol describing primary outcomes of safety, tolerability and feasibility, with use, craving and MRI measures as secondary and exploratory outcomes.

Conclusion The authors state that if the pilot proves safe, feasible and shows preliminary efficacy, it would justify larger studies and could offer a scalable short-stay TMS model.
