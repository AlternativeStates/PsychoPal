Aperiodic Exponent as an EEG Marker of Interoceptive Attention during Gastric Fullness

Sabrina Liu, Nandini Datta, James Lock, Todd P. Coleman
bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.64898/2026.11.09.765525
PreprintSpectral / aperiodic / waveformBrain–body physiology

Background Gastric interoceptive attention to internal, bodily signals such as hunger, satiety, and fullness are important for regulating feeding behaviors.

Methods Specifically, understanding cortical representations of self-perceived gastric fullness have yet to be studied in humans.

Results Simultaneous measures of the stomach and heart demonstrate increased vagal drive to peripheral organs. We further find that changes in cortical aperiodic exponent patterns are associated with changes in the frequency of gastric oscillations.

Conclusion Overall, this suggests that the EEG aperiodic exponent may be a useful noninvasive biomarker for assessing gastric interoceptive attention.
