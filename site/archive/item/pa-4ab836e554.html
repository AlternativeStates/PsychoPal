Ketamine promotes strategic and efficient approach-avoidance conflict resolution.

Stefan Vestring, Samuel Pfauth, Viktoria Galuba, Gabriel Burgos Kim, Dominik R Bach, Felix Mülsch, Feres Mocayar Marón, Katharina Domschke, Dominique Endres, Claus Normann, Juan Carlos Baldermann
Communications medicine · 2026-11-20 · doi:10.1038/s43856-026-01998-5
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modelling#ketamine#approach-avoidance#behaviour

Aim Whether ketamine promotes more strategic and efficient approach-avoidance behaviour in healthy adults, compared with lorazepam and no medication.

Methods Randomised crossover study in 34 healthy participants given control, intranasal ketamine 50 mg, or oral lorazepam 1 mg, with a dynamic approach-avoidance conflict task and mixed-effects models.

Results Ketamine produced 57% higher token gains than control (β = 0.09, p < 0.001) and 33% higher gains than lorazepam (β = 0.05, p = 0.009). Responses were more guided by outcome value and showed faster approach and withdrawal.

Conclusion Ketamine was associated with more efficient, value-sensitive approach-avoidance behaviour than control and lorazepam in healthy participants; assessors were blinded but participants were not.

Why it matters for you: Relevant to your ketamine plasticity work, showing a behavioural readout of ketamine action that could be paired with EEG or PET markers.
