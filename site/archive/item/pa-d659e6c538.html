From Rapid Synaptic Events to Lasting Antidepressant States: Convergent and Divergent Mechanisms of Ketamine and Psychedelics.

Jihye Kim, Manoela V Fogaca, Hermany Munguba, Marco Pignatelli, Jimcy Platholi, Samuel C Woodburn, Zhenzhong Zack Ma 马振中
The Journal of neuroscience : the official journal of the Society for Neuroscience · 2026-11-11 · doi:10.1523/jneurosci.1198-26.2026
Ketamine / esketamineDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)Peer reviewed

Background Rapid-acting antidepressants have challenged the long-standing view that therapeutic improvement in major depressive disorder requires chronic treatment with monoaminergic agents.

Results We first discuss hippocampal mechanisms underlying ketamine action, which include brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)/tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB)-dependent homeostatic potentiation, TrkB cross talk with metabotropic glutamate receptor 5 (mGluR5) at CA3→CA1 synapses, extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK)-dual-specificity phosphatase 6 (DUSP6)-mediated extension of synaptic metaplasticity, and BDNF release at mossy fiber synapses and dentate gyrus neuronal maturation.

Conclusion Together, these studies suggest that antidepressant efficacy arises not from a single pathway or brain region but from coordinated, temporally staged plasticity across molecules, synapses, cells, and circuits.
