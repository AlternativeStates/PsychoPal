Pharmacological Inhibition of MAPK Signaling Attenuates LSD-Induced Hallucinogenic-like Effects and Differentially Modulates Persistent Behavioral and Structural Plasticity in Mice

Jessica L. Maltman, Michael Fiorillo, Alexey Kozlenkov, Jennifer T. Wolstenholme, Mario de la Fuente Revenga, Javier González‐Maeso
ACS Chemical Neuroscience · 2026-11-23 · doi:10.1021/acschemneuro.6c00716
PsilocybinLSDDMTPre-clinicalPeer reviewedPre-clinical / invasive#lsd#mapk signalling#dendritic spines

Aim To test whether MAPK/ERK signalling contributes to the acute and persistent effects of LSD in male mice.

Methods Male mice received LSD with the brain-penetrant MEK inhibitor SL327 at several doses. Outcomes included immediate early gene expression, head-twitch response, forced-swim immobility and dendritic spine density in frontal cortex, with some analyses of beta-arrestin-2 knockouts.

Results SL327 dose-dependently attenuated LSD-induced c-Fos, Egr-1 and Egr-2 expression and markedly reduced acute head twitches without altering locomotion. LSD reduced thin spine density at 24 h and 7 days and increased mushroom spine density at 7 days; MAPK inhibition prevented the thin spine reduction.

Conclusion MAPK/ERK signalling contributes to psychedelic-specific transcription and acute and delayed LSD responses, with a spine-subtype-dependent role in cortical plasticity. Male-only sampling limits generalisation to females.

Why it matters for you: Speaks directly to LSD's plasticity and 5-HT2A signalling, the neuroplastogen questions behind your multimodal plasticity work.
