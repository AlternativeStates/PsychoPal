Testing the impact of cannabinoid stimulation on fatty acid amide hydrolase binding in the human brain: pilot [11C]CURB PET study

Raesham Mahmood, Shahtaj S. Dheda, Claudia Poluga, Tina McCluskey, Hsiang-Yuan Lin, Matthew E. Sloan, Rachel Fynvola Tyndale, Lucas Narciso, Jerry J. Warsh, Tony Peter George, Bernard Le Foll, Stefan M. Kloiber, Isabelle Boileau
Journal of Cannabis Research · 2026-11-09 · doi:10.1186/s42238-026-00531-8
PETSubstance use (other)Healthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedBrain stimulationMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)

Background Whether repeated exposure to cannabinoid receptor agonists is associated with changes in FAAH levels in the human brain remains unknown.

Methods In this exploratory Phase IV pilot study, ten healthy adults completed two positron emission tomography scans with the FAAH radiotracer [ 11 C]CURB: a baseline scan and a second scan following a 7-day oral …

Results Nabilone produced subjective effects, including increased marijuana-like drug effects ( p = 0.004), reductions in self-reported energy ( p = 0.02) and clear-headedness ( p = 0.01). No detectable mean change in FAAH was observed across brain regions following seven days of nabilone administration ( p = 0.357).

Conclusion These findings provide preliminary evidence regarding FAAH following repeated cannabinoid receptor agonist exposure and require confirmation in larger, controlled studies.
