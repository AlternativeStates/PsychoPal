Anesthesia via Dissociative Overload: A Unified Theoretical Framework Linking DMT and Ketamine Through Sigma-1 Convergence, Entropic Saturation, and Workspace Flooding

M. Hawarey
· 2026-11-03 · doi:10.65737/airlsm2026698
DMTKetamine / esketaminePeer reviewedInformation theory / complexityComputational / whole-brain modellingPreprocessing / source / tools

Background General anesthesia has long been understood as a unitary phenomenon driven by neural suppression — a reduction in cortical activity, entropy, and complexity that renders the brain unable to sustain conscious processing.

Methods Here, we propose a novel unified framework for “Anesthesia via Dissociative Overload,” arguing that DMT and ketamine achieve unresponsiveness not by silencing neural activity but by saturating cortical information processing beyond the capacity …

Results The Dissociative Overload model reframes general anesthesia as a family of states unified by the failure to model external reality, achievable through either information deprivation or information saturation.

Conclusion While the framework is empirically grounded for ketamine, its extension to DMT-induced anesthesia constitutes a central prediction awaiting direct experimental validation.
