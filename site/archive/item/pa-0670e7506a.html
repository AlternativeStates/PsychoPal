A Neandertal-derived genetic variant reduces occipital brain volume in present-day humans

Hugo Zeberg, Svante Pääbo
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · 2026-11-16 · doi:10.1073/pnas.2624837123
Psychosis / schizophreniaClinicalPeer reviewed#neandertal#occipital volume#schizophrenia genetics

Aim Whether a Neandertal-derived genetic variant influences occipital brain volume and schizophrenia risk in present-day humans.

Methods Genetic association analysis linking a Neandertal-inherited variant near DAAM1 to occipital lobe volume, gene expression, schizophrenia risk and cognitive measures.

Results Carriers had occipital volumes about 0.85 cm³ smaller per gene copy. Homozygous carriers showed almost 10% lower schizophrenia risk, with little or no association with cognition or schooling.

Conclusion The authors conclude the variant alters brain anatomy and lowers schizophrenia risk with little association with general cognitive ability.

Why it matters for you: A neuroscientist may find the link between an archaic variant, occipital anatomy and schizophrenia risk of interest.
