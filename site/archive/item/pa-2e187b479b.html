A shared slowing axis and phenotype-specific rhythms link clinical EEG to FDG-PET dementia phenotypes

N. Satyadev, V. Gogineni, N. Corriveau-Lecavalier, G. Zhang, B. Brinkmann, Y. Varatharajah, G. S. Day, J. L. Whitwell, K. A. Josephs, K. Kantarci, B. F. Boeve, R. C. Petersen, M. Breakspear, V. J. Lowe, D. T. Jones

Dementia / Alzheimer'sPreprintSpectral / aperiodic / waveformPreprocessing / source / toolsMultimodal imaging (PET/DTI)

Background Early and accurate identification of neurodegenerative disorders has notable implications for treatment and quality of life.

Methods Whereas neuroimaging-based biomarkers can accurately identify neurodegenerative subtypes, EEG represents a low-cost alternative that could expand access to dementia care.

Results Four factors reflecting neurophysiological oscillations (posterior alpha, Rolandic beta, frontotemporal delta-theta and posterior theta-slow alpha) varied significantly across phenotypes, with attenuation of faster and augmentation of slower rhythms most pronounced in dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), dysexecutive Alzheimer disease (AD) and posterior cortical atrophy, whereas factors dominated by eye-movement and muscle artifact showed no robust …

Conclusion Thus, data-driven decomposition of clinical EEG separates oscillatory activity from artifact into factors that track metabolic phenotype, regional metabolism and cognition, capturing both overall slowing and phenotype-specific patterns.
