Unmixing the psychedelic connectome: Brain network traits of psilocybin.

Krishna Prasad Bhavaraju, Natasha L Mason, Pablo Mallaroni, Dietmar Heinke, Stefan W Toennes, Johannes G Ramaekers, Enrico Amico
Network neuroscience (Cambridge, Mass.) · 2026-10-22 · doi:10.1162/netn.a.594
PsilocybinfMRIHealthy volunteersClinicalPeer reviewedConnectivity / synchronyComputational / whole-brain modelling#psilocybin#connectome#fmri

Aim Test whether the psilocybin-induced connectome is a composite of dissociable connectivity patterns rather than one global change.

Methods Connectome independent component analysis with multilevel resampling applied to resting-state fMRI from healthy volunteers given psilocybin, relating traits to plasma psilocin and a divergent thinking task.

Results Two empirically uncorrelated functional connectivity traits emerged: one scaled with plasma psilocin concentration, and a second was associated with poorer performance on a visual divergent thinking task.

Conclusion The acute psilocybin state appears to involve co-occurring, dissociable connectivity patterns, supporting decompositional connectomic approaches.

Why it matters for you: Brings a data-driven decomposition of psychedelic connectivity that fits your neurodynamics and whole-brain modelling interests.
