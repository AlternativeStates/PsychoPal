Cannabidiol prevents fear extinction deficits caused by single prolonged stress in rats: possible involvement of serotoninergic, cannabinoid, and nitric mechanisms.

Carla Vila-Verde, Mariana G Fronza, Daniela L Uliana, Leonardo B Resstel, Francisco S Guimarães, Sabrina F Lisboa
Neuropharmacology · 2026-11-06 · doi:10.1016/j.neuropharm.2026.111256
PTSDPre-clinicalPeer reviewedComputational / whole-brain modellingBrain–body physiology

Background Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) involves impaired extinction learning, autonomic and neuroendocrine dysregulation, and first-line selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) show limited efficacy.

Methods We investigated effects and mechanisms of cannabidiol (CBD) on fear deficits induced by the single prolonged stress (SPS) model in male Wistar rats.

Results Repeated CBD (5 and 10 mg/kg, 7 days) prevented extinction memory impairments, and these effects were attenuated by the 5-HT1A antagonist WAY-100635 and the CB1 antagonist AM281.

Conclusion These findings indicate that CBD prevents SPS-induced extinction deficits through 5-HT1A- and CB1-dependent mechanisms, identify nNOS as a candidate downstream substrate, and support CBD as a multi-target candidate for PTSD treatment.
