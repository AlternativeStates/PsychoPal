Transcranial magnetic stimulation to the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex modulates single-neuron activity in humans.

Charles W Dickey, Umair Hassan, Hiroto Kawasaki, Ariane E Rhone, Christopher C Cline, Matthew A Howard, Nicholas T Trapp, Aaron D Boes, Joel I Berger, Corey J Keller
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America · 2026-11-02 · doi:10.1073/pnas.2610803123
TMS / TMS-EEGDepression (incl. TRD/MDD)ClinicalPeer reviewedERP / MMN / predictive codingConnectivity / synchronyBrain stimulationPreprocessing / source / toolsPre-clinical / invasive#tms#single neurons#dlpfc

Aim To determine how single-pulse TMS to the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex affects single-neuron activity in deep cortical and subcortical structures in humans.

Methods Intracranial microelectrode recordings of 185 single neurons in four neurosurgical patients during single-pulse TMS to left dlPFC, with artifact cleaning enabling spike resolution from 8 ms, alongside TMS-evoked potentials from depth macroelectrodes.

Results Single-pulse TMS elicited time-locked responses in 46% of neurons (26% facilitated, 19% suppressed, 1% both), in putamen and thalamus among other regions. Putative inhibitory striato-thalamic neurons were facilitated, while excitatory and limbic neurons were suppressed with slower time courses.

Conclusion The authors propose dlPFC TMS recruits many neurons across bilateral cortical and subcortical regions, selectively boosting inhibitory firing in executive networks while suppressing limbic drive. Data come from four patients.

Why it matters for you: Directly addresses how TMS perturbs neural circuits at single-neuron resolution, central to your TMS-EEG and whole-brain modelling interests.
